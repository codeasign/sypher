Nine modules compress into one page you can read right before walking into a real negotiation. Nothing here is new. The whole point of a cheat sheet is that you shouldn't have to remember nine separate lessons under pressure, just these checks.

## Before you speak

- What's my walk-away point, written down, not just felt?
- What number am I opening with, and what's my one-line reason for it?
- Am I asking for more than my actual target, on purpose?

## While the conversation is happening

- Did I speak the first number, or did I just react to theirs?
- If I'm giving something up, what am I getting in return?
- Am I filling silence out of nerves, or letting it sit?
- What's the interest behind their position, not just the position itself?

## When you hit resistance

"No budget":
> "Is that for the whole thing, or could a smaller version fit what's approved now?"

A wall of silence after your offer:
> [say nothing, count to five, let them respond first]

A lowball counteroffer:
> "That's below what I can work with. Based on ___, I need this closer to ___."

Being asked to concede with nothing offered back:
> "I can do that if we can also agree on ___."

A flat "that's final":
> "What's driving that number? Trying to find something that works for both of us."

## The script bank

Opening with an anchor:
> "Based on ___, I'm targeting ___."

Trading instead of conceding:
> "If you can do ___, then I can do ___."

Asking about the real constraint:
> "What matters most here, is it the price, the timeline, or something else?"

Holding your walk-away point under pressure:
> "I hear that, but I can't go below ___ given ___."

Closing when terms are agreed:
> "So to confirm, we've landed on ___. Does that match what you have?"

## Where to start

Don't try to use all nine skills in your next negotiation. Pick the walk-away point first, it's the one that protects you from every other mistake, because a clear floor stops pressure from moving you somewhere you didn't choose. Add anchoring next, then the rest as you find you need them.

The goal was never to win every negotiation. It's to stop accepting the first number by default, and start treating every price, deadline, and request as a conversation instead of a decision someone else already made for you.
