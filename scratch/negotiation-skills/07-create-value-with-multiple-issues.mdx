---
title: "Create Value With Multiple Issues"
order: 7
---

Negotiations get stuck when both sides argue over one number. A client wants a lower fee. You want the full fee. If price is the only movable part, one person must lose for the other to gain.

Most agreements contain more than one issue: price, timing, scope, payment date, quantity, support, risk, publicity, location, or responsibility. The two sides rarely value every issue equally. That difference creates trades.

## List what can move

Before discussing concessions, write every term that could change. For freelance work, the list might include:

- Total fee
- Deposit and payment timing
- Delivery date
- Number of concepts or revision rounds
- Support after delivery
- Permission to show the work in a portfolio

Do not assume the other side values them as you do. Ask:

> "If the fee stayed where it is, which other term would make this easier for you?"

The answer may reveal that cash timing matters more than total cost.

## Trade low-cost value for high-value terms

A good trade gives the other side something they value more than you do and receives something you value more than they do.

> "I can split the invoice across two months if we keep the full scope and sign by Friday."

Payment timing may be cheap for you and useful to the client. The signed scope may matter more to you. Both sides improve their position without pretending the price gap vanished.

## Offer packages instead of one demand

Packages reveal priorities and prevent a fight over a single number:

> "Option A is $3,000 with delivery this month and one revision. Option B is $3,600 with delivery next week and three revisions."

If the client chooses speed, you learn speed matters. If they choose the smaller package, you protect the value of the work by reducing scope rather than discounting the same work.

Change more than one term between packages, but keep each package clear enough to compare.

## PRACTICE

**1. Expand the issue list.** You are negotiating a job offer. Name five terms besides salary.

**Suggested answer:** Start date, remote days, title, signing payment, vacation, training budget, review date, schedule, and responsibilities are all possible terms. Choose the ones you genuinely value.

**2. Build a trade.** A customer wants a 15 percent discount. You care about payment speed and contract length. Write an `if, then` response.

**Suggested answer:** "If you can pay upfront and commit to six months, then I can reduce the monthly fee by 10 percent." The discount buys terms that matter to you.

**3. Offer two packages.** A client can choose between low cost and fast delivery. Write both options.

**Suggested answer:** "Option A is $2,000 with delivery in four weeks. Option B is $2,700 with delivery next Friday." The client chooses the tradeoff instead of asking you to provide both advantages for one price.
