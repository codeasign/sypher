Most people treat negotiation as something that happens at a car dealership. It happens far more often than that: asking for a raise, splitting rent with a roommate, pushing back on a deadline, buying a used couch, telling a client the scope changed. This course treats it as a skill you use weekly, not a special event.

## Who this is for

Anyone who has ever accepted the first number because asking felt awkward. You don't need a business background or a loud personality. Some of the best negotiators in this course's examples are quiet, methodical, and slightly nervous the whole time. That's fine. The skill is in what you say, not how confident you sound saying it.

## What you'll learn

By the end you will be able to:

- Set a walk-away point before a conversation starts, so pressure in the room can't move it.
- Open with a number instead of waiting to react to theirs.
- Ask for more than feels comfortable, on purpose.
- Trade concessions instead of giving them away for free.
- Sit through silence instead of filling it with a worse offer.
- Read what the other side actually needs, not just what they said.

## How it works

Each module covers one move:

1. What most people do instead, and why it costs them.
2. A realistic bad example next to a better one.
3. One line on why the better version works.
4. A short "TRY IT" so you practice the exact wording.

Exercises are rewrites, not quizzes. The suggested answer sits right below each one so you can compare immediately. Your version doesn't need to match it word for word; it needs to hit the same target.

Read one module before a negotiation you actually have coming up. A rent renewal, a job offer, a contractor quote. The skill sticks when you use it on something real within a day or two, not when you read all ten modules back to back and file them away.

## TRY IT

Before Module 2, write down one number you've accepted recently without asking for anything different: a salary, a price, a deadline, a rent increase.

**Suggested answer:** There's no single right answer here; the point is noticing. Most people can name one within ten seconds, which is exactly why this course exists.
