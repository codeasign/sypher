Negotiation happens whenever two people want different terms and still need an agreement. That includes salary, rent, project scope, deadlines, purchases, household responsibilities, and the awkward moment when someone asks for more than you can give. This course teaches you how to prepare, ask, trade, respond, and close without treating the other person as an opponent.

## Who this is for

Anyone who has accepted the first number because asking felt awkward. You do not need a business background or a loud personality. Preparation, careful questions, and clear terms matter more than sounding fearless.

## What you'll learn

By the end you will be able to:

- Set a walk-away point before a conversation starts, so pressure in the room can't move it.
- Separate your alternative, your walk-away point, and your target.
- Estimate the bargaining range and decide when speaking first helps.
- Make an ambitious request that you can defend.
- Trade concessions instead of giving them away for free.
- Combine several issues so both sides have more ways to agree.
- Sit through silence instead of filling it with a worse offer.
- Read what the other side actually needs, not just what they said.
- Respond to pressure tactics, break deadlocks, and confirm the final terms.

## How it works

Each module covers one part of the conversation:

1. The common mistake and why it weakens the negotiation.
2. A realistic bad example next to a better one.
3. One line on why the better version works.
4. A short `PRACTICE` section so you write the exact wording.

Exercises are rewrites, not quizzes. The suggested answer sits right below each one so you can compare immediately. Your version doesn't need to match it word for word; it needs to hit the same target.

Read one module before a negotiation you actually have coming up. It could be a rent renewal, a job offer, a contractor quote, or a deadline. Use the worksheets and scripts on that situation. The goal is not to recite terminology. It is to enter with choices instead of making every decision under pressure.

## PRACTICE

**1. Notice the default.** Write down one term you accepted recently without asking for anything different: a salary, price, deadline, responsibility, or rent increase.

**Suggested answer:** There is no single right answer. The point is to notice a negotiation you may have treated as a fixed decision, then identify one term you could have asked about.

**2. Name more than money.** A client asks you to lower your fee. List two terms besides price that you could discuss.

**Suggested answer:** "I could discuss a later delivery date and a smaller scope." Payment timing, revision limits, contract length, and support hours could also create room without cutting the fee alone.
