---
title: "Anchor First"
order: 3
---

A recruiter asks, "What are your salary expectations?" Most people answer with a range, low end padded down "to be safe," and the offer that comes back sits near the bottom of that range. Not because the company is stingy. Because the first number spoken pulls every number after it toward itself, and the candidate just handed that pull to the other side for free.

This is called anchoring. Whoever names a number first sets the gravity for the rest of the conversation, even when both sides know the number is just an opening move.

## Bad: waiting to hear their number

> "I'd want to see what you had in mind first, honestly whatever's fair."

This feels humble. It also hands the anchor away. Their "fair" is built around their budget, not your value, and now you're negotiating up from their starting point instead of down from yours.

## Better: naming a number, backed by something

> "Based on this role and my experience with similar systems, I'm targeting $95,000 to $100,000."

Two things happened here. You spoke first, and you attached a reason, so the number doesn't sound plucked from the air.

## Anchor slightly aggressive, not absurd

An anchor that's obviously unreasonable, asking a landlord to cut rent by 40%, gets laughed off and the whole negotiation resets around your credibility instead of the number. Aim for the high end of what you could realistically defend if pressed. For salary, that's usually the top of the market range for the role, not the ceiling of what exists anywhere.

## When they anchor first

If they get the number out before you do, don't just react to it. Restate your own reference point before responding: "I was actually looking at closer to $X based on the scope we discussed." You can't unspend their anchor, but you can plant a second one right next to it.

## TRY IT

**1. Write an anchor with a reason attached.** You're selling a used car listed nowhere yet. Comparable cars go for $8,000 to $9,500. Write your opening asking price and the one-line reason behind it.

**Suggested answer:** "$9,200, it's the low-mileage version of what's selling for $8,000 to $9,500 nearby." Anchored near the top, justified by a specific detail.

**2. Rewrite the passive opener.** A client asks what your rate is for a project. Rewrite this: "Oh, whatever you think is reasonable works for me."

**Suggested answer:** "For a project like this, I'm at $3,500, based on similar scopes I've delivered." Speaks first, attaches a reason.

**3. Respond to their anchor.** A supplier opens with "$15 per unit." You'd budgeted for $11. Write your response.

**Suggested answer:** "I was working from $10 to $11 based on other quotes I've gotten for this volume. Where's the room between that and $15?" Plants your own anchor instead of just reacting to theirs.
