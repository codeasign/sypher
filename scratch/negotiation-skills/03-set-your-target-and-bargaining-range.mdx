Your walk-away point protects you from a bad agreement. It does not tell you what to ask for. For that, you need a target and a reasonable picture of where agreement might be possible.

Four numbers or outcomes belong on your preparation page:

1. Your alternative if there is no deal.
2. Your walk-away point, where the deal stops beating that alternative.
3. Your target, the result you are trying to reach.
4. Your opening request, which leaves room to move toward the target.

These are different. If a freelance project is worth taking at $3,000, you might target $4,000 and open at $4,500. Treating $3,000 as all three numbers makes every concession push you toward a bad deal.

## Estimate the bargaining range

The bargaining range is the overlap between what you can accept and what the other side can accept. Negotiators often call it the zone of possible agreement, or ZOPA. You rarely know its exact edges, but you can estimate them from comparable offers, budgets, past agreements, deadlines, and direct questions.

Suppose you will not sell a used car below $8,000, and the buyer will not pay above $9,000. Agreement may exist between those points. If the buyer cannot exceed $7,500, changing your wording will not create an overlap. You need to change another term or walk away.

## Use ranges as research, not camouflage

A range can help you think before the conversation. Saying, "Anything from $70,000 to $90,000 works," gives the other person a reason to focus on the lower number. If you share a range, make both ends acceptable and explain what changes within it.

> "For this scope, I see the fee between $4,000 and $4,800, depending on the number of revision rounds and the delivery date."

Now the range is tied to terms instead of uncertainty.

## Write the page before you negotiate

Under pressure, people move numbers without remembering why. Put your alternative, walk-away point, target, opening, and the facts supporting them on one page. If new information changes a number, change it deliberately.

## PRACTICE

**1. Separate the four outcomes.** You would keep your current job rather than accept less than $70,000. A fair result is $78,000, and similar roles reach $84,000. Write your alternative, walk-away point, target, and opening request.

**Suggested answer:** Alternative: keep the current job. Walk-away point: $70,000, assuming the other terms are comparable. Target: $78,000. Opening: about $82,000 to $84,000 with evidence from similar roles.

**2. Find the missing overlap.** You will not sell below $500. The buyer cannot pay above $450. What should you do next?

**Suggested answer:** There is no price overlap. Ask whether timing, delivery, accessories, or payment structure can create value. If no other term closes the gap, do not force an agreement.

**3. Make a range meaningful.** Rewrite: "I charge somewhere between $1,000 and $2,000."

**Suggested answer:** "The fee is $1,400 for the standard scope or $1,900 with two extra concepts and a one-week turnaround." Each number now buys a clear package.
