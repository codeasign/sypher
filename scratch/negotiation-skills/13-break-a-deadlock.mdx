---
title: "Break a Deadlock"
order: 13
---

A deadlock means the current version of the agreement does not work. Repeating the same demand more firmly rarely changes that. Your next move is to find out whether the gap is about information, one fixed term, or the whole deal.

## Summarize what is already settled

Start by shrinking the disagreement:

> "We agree on the scope, delivery date, and payment schedule. The remaining gap is $800 on the total fee. Is that how you see it?"

The summary prevents settled issues from drifting back into the argument. It may also reveal that you were solving different problems.

## Change the shape of the deal

If one term cannot move, bring back other issues. Reduce scope, change timing, split risk, adjust quantity, or create two packages.

> "If the budget cannot exceed $4,000, we can remove the research phase, or keep the full scope and move delivery into next month. Which is more useful?"

Do not cut price while quietly promising the same work. That creates an agreement on paper and resentment in practice.

## Use a fair way to settle uncertainty

Sometimes the gap exists because neither side knows what will happen. Agree on a test, review point, or rule instead of arguing over predictions.

> "Let's use the lower rate for the first month. If the weekly volume exceeds 20 hours, the higher rate applies from month two."

The rule ties the result to something both sides can observe.

## Know when to pause or stop

A pause gives both sides time to check facts and alternatives. State when you will return and what each person will examine.

> "We're repeating the same points. Let's pause until Thursday. I'll confirm the delivery cost, and you can check the budget ceiling."

If the walk-away points do not overlap and no other term closes the gap, ending the negotiation is a valid result. A forced agreement that one side cannot keep is not progress.

## PRACTICE

**1. Shrink the disagreement.** You and a contractor agree on materials, start date, and scope but differ on payment timing. Write a summary.

**Suggested answer:** "We agree on the work, materials, and Monday start. The only open point is whether the final half is paid at the start or after inspection. Is that right?"

**2. Change the shape.** A client cannot meet your full fee. Offer one smaller-scope option and one later-delivery option.

**Suggested answer:** "We can meet the budget by removing the training session, or keep the full package and deliver next month when I can schedule it at the standard rate. Which tradeoff fits better?"

**3. Build a review rule.** Your manager will not approve a permanent remote day because they are unsure how it will affect coverage. Propose a test.

**Suggested answer:** "Could we test Wednesdays remotely for six weeks, track missed coverage, and review it on October 30?" A limited test replaces competing predictions with an observable result.
