title: "Use Silence"
order: 9
---

You name your number. The other person pauses. Two seconds feel like twenty. Your brain starts narrating: they think it's too high, say something, fix it, soften it. So you do: "...but obviously that's flexible, we can talk about it." You just discounted your own offer, and nobody asked you to.

Silence after an offer is not automatically a problem to solve. The other person may be thinking, checking your confidence, or deciding what to say. Filling the pause can make you give away ground before hearing a response.

## Bad: filling the gap

> "I need this delivered by Friday."
> [pause]
> "I mean, Monday could maybe work too, if Friday's tight, whatever's easier for you honestly."

The deadline moved from Friday to "whatever's easier" in about four seconds, with zero pushback from the other person. The silence did the work; you did it to yourself.

## Better: letting it sit

> "I need this delivered by Friday."
> [pause]
> [says nothing, waits]

Whoever speaks next often reveals more than whoever spoke first. Maybe they agree. Maybe they counter with a real constraint: "Friday's tough, Monday morning is realistic." Now you're negotiating against an actual reason instead of an imagined objection you invented to fill the quiet.

## What to do with your hands instead of your mouth

If the silence feels uncomfortable, count to five in your head before speaking again. Take a breath, glance at your notes, or take a sip of water. The pause gives the other person room to respond and gives you time to choose your next words.

## This goes both ways

If you ask someone a hard question and they go quiet, resist finishing their sentence for them or offering an easier out. "Take your time" and an actual pause gets you a more honest answer than rescuing them from having to give one.

## PRACTICE

**1. Rewrite the self-discount.** You just said: "I'd like $80 for the piece." Then, unprompted: "But $60 is fine too, really." Rewrite this exchange so the silence does the work instead.

**Suggested answer:** "I'd like $80 for the piece." Then stop talking and let the buyer respond first, whether that's an agreement, a counter, or a question.

**2. Spot the trap.** A colleague asks for a status update you don't have good news about. You pause to think. They jump in: "It's fine, don't worry about it, I figured." What did their filling the silence cost them?

**Suggested answer:** They lost the chance to hear the actual, possibly worse, update, because they answered their own question before you could.

**3. Practice the count.** Write out a sentence you'd say to open a negotiation, then write "[pause, count to five]" after it, then write what you'd do with your hands during that pause.

**Suggested answer:** Any concrete physical action (glancing at notes, breathing, taking a sip of water) is the right answer; the point is having something to do besides talk.
