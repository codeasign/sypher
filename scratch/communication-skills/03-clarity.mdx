> "Hi! Sorry to bother you, I know you're super busy. I was just wondering if maybe at some point, no rush at all, the report could possibly be ready? Only if you have time of course!"

That message took the writer thirty seconds and will cost the reader three days, because "no rush" usually means "I needed it yesterday" and the reader knows it.

Now the same request:

> "Can you send me the report by Thursday?"

Eleven words. The reader knows exactly what to do and when.

## Where clarity dies

Three habits bury a clear request under noise:

**Hedging.** "Just wondering", "maybe", "possibly", "if that's okay". Hedges feel polite but they transfer your uncertainty to the reader, who now has to guess how urgent this is. One hedge is politeness. Five is fog.

**Vague quantities.** "Soon", "a few", "ASAP", "when you get a chance". ASAP means different things to different people. Thursday means Thursday. If a date, number, or name exists, use it.

**Burying the ask.** Three paragraphs of context, then the actual request in the last line. Lead with the point: "Can we move Friday's demo to Tuesday? Context below." Readers decide in the first sentence whether to keep reading.

## One ask per message

Two requests in one paragraph means one gets dropped. Split them into two messages or number them:

> "Two things: 1) Approve the invoice by Friday. 2) Send me Priya's contact so I can set up the call."

Clear writing moves the effort from the reader to the writer, which is where it belongs.

## TRY IT

**1. Rewrite the vague request:**

> "Hey, whenever you get a sec, could you maybe take a look at the thing I sent last week? No pressure!"

**Suggested answer:** "Can you review the design file I sent Monday by Wednesday? Flag anything that looks off." Notice what changed: "whenever you get a sec" became a deadline, "the thing" became the design file, "last week" became Monday, and "no pressure" became a concrete action (flag issues).

**2. Make it specific.** Your landlord texts: "The rent will be increasing soon." Rewrite it the way you'd want to receive it.

**Suggested answer:** "Heads-up: rent increases from $850 to $900 starting October 1. New leases will be sent by September 15." Same news, zero guessing about amount, date, or next step.

**3. Lead with the ask.** Reorder this into one clear opening line: "We've been getting a lot of support tickets since Tuesday, mostly about password resets, and engineering thinks the new release changed the login flow somehow, so can we roll it back this afternoon?"

**Suggested answer:** "Can we roll back this afternoon's release? Since Tuesday we've had a spike in password-reset tickets, likely caused by its login flow change." The request now comes first; the evidence follows it.
