title: "What Good Communication Actually Looks Like"
order: 2
---

Good communication is not fancy vocabulary or a smooth delivery. It's the size of the gap between what you meant and what the other person understood. Skilled communicators keep that gap small. Everyone else talks past each other and calls it a personality clash.

## Intent vs impact

You judge yourself by intent ("I didn't mean it rudely"). Other people judge you by impact ("that read as rude"). Both are true at once, which is why "that's not what I meant" fixes nothing after the fact. The fix happens before you speak: check how your words will land, not just whether they're accurate.

Compare two ways to tell a client the same news:

> "We're running a bit behind schedule due to some unforeseen issues, but we're confident it'll be fine."

> "The launch moves from Monday to Thursday. Two bugs came up in testing. I'll send a fix plan today."

The first protects the speaker's feelings. The second respects the listener's need to know what changed, why, and what happens next.

## Three habits that close the gap

**Decide the outcome first.** Before any conversation, finish this sentence: "After this, they should know ___ and do ___." If you can't fill the blank, you're not ready to talk yet.

**Watch their reaction, not just your words.** Confused face? Interrupted you to change topics? Nodding but asking nothing? Those are signals your message isn't landing. Adjust mid-conversation instead of repeating yourself louder.

**Close the loop.** End with a quick confirmation: "So you'll review the draft by Wednesday?" Ten seconds now beats a week of "I thought YOU were doing that."

## TRY IT

**1. Spot the missing outcome.** A teammate messages you: "Hey, quick question about the presentation." Then nothing for an hour. What did they want that never happened?

**Suggested answer:** They wanted an answer, but they never asked anything. A better version: "Quick question about the presentation: do you want slide 12 kept in or cut?" Now the reply takes ten seconds.

**2. Name the gap.** Your friend says: "I'm fine with whatever restaurant you pick." You pick one; they're visibly unhappy all evening. Where was the gap between intent and impact?

**Suggested answer:** Their words said "whatever", their intent was probably "I'd like a say, but I don't want to seem difficult". Closing the gap beforehand takes one direct question: "Any cuisine you're definitely not in the mood for?"
