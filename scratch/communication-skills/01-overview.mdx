Most communication advice sounds nice and changes nothing. This course skips the advice and gives you skills: specific things to say, in situations you actually face, with examples of what works and what doesn't.

## Who this is for

Anyone. Students, job seekers, engineers, managers, freelancers, teachers. Nothing here assumes a technical background or a particular job. If you talk to people (or message them), this applies.

## What you'll learn

By the end you will be able to:

- Say what you mean in one clear sentence instead of five hedged ones.
- Listen without rehearsing your reply, and prove you heard the other person.
- Ask questions that prevent expensive misunderstandings.
- Speak up in meetings without sounding either timid or pushy.
- Say no, disagree, and deliver bad news without damaging the relationship.
- Write emails and chat messages people can act on the first time they read them.

## How it works

Each module teaches one skill in a few minutes:

1. A short explanation of what most people get wrong.
2. A bad example and a better example, side by side.
3. One line on why the better version works.
4. A short "TRY IT" practice so the skill sticks.

Exercises are rewrites and responses, not quizzes. Each one is followed by its suggested answer right below it, so you can compare immediately. Your answer doesn't have to match the suggestion; it has to be clear.

Do one module at a time and use the skill the same day, at work or at home. That's where it becomes yours.

## TRY IT

Warm up with one rewrite before Module 2. Make this message answerable:

> "Hey, whenever you get a sec, could you maybe look at the thing I sent?"

**Suggested answer:** "Can you review the design file I sent Monday by Wednesday?" The vague "thing" became the design file, "whenever you get a sec" became a deadline, and the reader now knows exactly what to do.
