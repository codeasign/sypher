In a Raft cluster with 5 nodes, the leader has the following log: entries at indices 1-5 with terms [term=1, term=2, term=2, term=3, term=4]. A follower has entries at indices 1-4 with terms [term=1, term=2, term=2, term=2] (it missed the term-3 entry at index 4 because it was partitioned during term 3). The leader sends AppendEntries with prevLogIndex=4 and prevLogTerm=3, carrying the new entry at index 5. Will the follower accept this request? Explain why or why not. Then describe what happens next.

**Answer:**

The follower checks whether its log has an entry at index 4 with term 3. Its entry at index 4 has term 2, so the consistency check fails and the follower rejects the request.

The leader then decrements nextIndex for this follower and retries with prevLogIndex=3, prevLogTerm=2, and it sends the entries from index 4 onward (the term-3 entry at index 4 and the term-4 entry at index 5). This time the follower's entry at index 3 has term 2, which matches, so the check passes. If any of the follower's existing entries conflicts with the incoming ones (index 4 has term 2 on the follower and term 3 in the new entries), the follower deletes the conflicting entry and everything after it, then appends the leader's entries.

After this exchange the follower's log is [term=1, term=2, term=2, term=3, term=4], identical to the leader's.

The key insight is the log matching property: if two logs have an entry with the same index and term, they are identical up to that point. The AppendEntries check enforces it by verifying only the immediate predecessor, and because every earlier append was checked the same way, a match at prevLogIndex guarantees a match everywhere before it. When the check fails, the leader walks backward until it finds a point where the logs agree, and then overwrites the follower's divergent suffix.
