### Netflix: Stateless Microservices at 300 Million Subscribers

Netflix operates one of the largest horizontally scaled systems in the world, serving over 300 million subscribers in more than 190 countries. Its video is delivered from its own content delivery network, and its control plane (browsing, playback authorization, recommendations) handles enormous volumes of API requests.

**The challenge**: Netflix's original architecture was a monolithic application backed by a relational database in its own data center. After a major database corruption incident in 2008 halted DVD shipments, Netflix decided it could not rely on a single vertically scaled stack. It moved to AWS and split the monolith into many independently deployable services.

**The solution**: Netflix built a stateless microservice architecture on AWS. Each microservice is horizontally scaled behind a load balancer. Netflix open-sourced several of the components that made this work:

- **Eureka** (service discovery): Every service registers itself on startup and deregisters on shutdown, and clients look up healthy instances from the registry. Eureka is itself replicated across availability zones.
- **Zuul** (API gateway): Routes requests to the appropriate backend service and handles cross-cutting concerns like authentication and routing at the edge. Zuul runs in an auto-scaling group behind a load balancer.
- **Hystrix** (circuit breaker): Service-to-service calls went through Hystrix, which provided circuit breakers, thread pool isolation, and fallbacks to prevent cascading failures. Hystrix has three states (CLOSED, OPEN, HALF-OPEN) and tracks failures in a rolling window (10 seconds by default). Netflix put it into maintenance mode in 2018 in favor of adaptive concurrency limits, and resilience4j is the usual replacement in new Java projects, but the pattern it popularized is unchanged.
- **Scryer** (predictive auto-scaling): A forecasting engine that predicts demand from historical patterns and provisions instances before the demand arrives, avoiding the several-minute lag of purely reactive scaling.
