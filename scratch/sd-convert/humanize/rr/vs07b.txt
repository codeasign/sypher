**Model answer**: "The working set is the data that receives most of the accesses. The hottest 60 GB serves 80% of queries, so caching only that gives an 80% hit ratio (the current 78% is consistent with 64 GB of RAM holding most of it). Adding the next 120 GB tier brings the hit ratio to 95% at 180 GB of cached data. Reaching 99% is much more expensive, because the last 5% of accesses are spread across 320 GB. To cut the miss rate from 5% to 1%, you must also cache 4/5 of that tier (about 256 GB), for a total of about 436 GB (assuming accesses are uniform within each tier). With headroom for connections and the OS, that means a 512 GB instance. Options: r7g.8xlarge (256 GB) at about $1,250/month reaches a hit ratio of roughly 95%, and r7g.16xlarge (512 GB) at about $2,500/month reaches 99%. Compared with the current r7g.2xlarge at about $310/month, going from 95% to 99% doubles the bill for four points of hit ratio, so the right answer depends on how latency-sensitive the workload is. This is the diminishing-returns curve of vertical scaling for memory."
