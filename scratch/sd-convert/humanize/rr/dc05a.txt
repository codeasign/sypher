### Netflix: A Counter Abstraction

Netflix has described a general-purpose "Distributed Counter Abstraction" in its engineering blog (2024). It exists because many teams need counters (how many times something was watched, how many experiments ran) and each needs a different trade-off between accuracy, latency, and cost. (The figures in the diagram below are illustrative.)

**Architecture**: The abstraction offers more than one counting mode:

- **Best-effort regional counters**: Increments go to a fast in-memory store (EVCache, Netflix's Memcached-based cache) within a region. They are cheap and low-latency, and they can under- or over-count, because an increment can be lost or a retry can be applied twice. They suit uses such as metrics and A/B test exposure counts, where an approximate number is enough.
- **Eventually consistent global counters**: Each increment is written as an event to a durable time-series store, and background processes roll the events up into a running total. Because the events are durable and each carries an identifier, retries are safe and the total converges to an accurate value, at the cost of a delay before the total is up to date.

**Trade-off**: The design makes the accuracy-versus-latency choice explicit. Cheap counters are fast but approximate, and accurate counters are built on an event log and are slightly delayed. Teams pick the mode that matches their need.

**Public reference**: Netflix Tech Blog, "Netflix's Distributed Counter Abstraction" (2024).
