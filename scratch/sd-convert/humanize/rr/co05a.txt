## What Usually Goes Wrong in Practice

Public postmortems and operator experience point to a consistent set of causes for consensus-cluster trouble, roughly in order of how often they are reported:

- **Slow or contended disks.** Every commit waits on an fsync, so disk latency spikes turn into leader changes and timeouts. This is the most common cause reported for etcd and similar systems.
- **CPU starvation, long GC pauses, and noisy neighbors.** A leader that cannot send heartbeats in time triggers an election even though nothing is broken.
- **Network jitter and partial partitions.** Packet loss and asymmetric failures cause missed heartbeats and, in ZooKeeper-style systems, session expirations that cascade into bursts of reconnections and writes.
- **Membership changes done wrongly.** Adding, removing, or replacing nodes carelessly can cost the cluster its quorum.
- **Unbounded growth.** Logs, snapshots, or databases that outgrow their disks or memory limits.

None of these is a flaw in the consensus algorithm. They are the operational conditions under which the algorithm's timeouts and quorum rules become visible.
