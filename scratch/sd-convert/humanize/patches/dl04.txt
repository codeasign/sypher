A leader election in the lock service cluster causes all locks to be re-evaluated. During the election window, no locks can be acquired or released. After the election, all leases that were held by the previous leader are invalidated.

**Root cause**: In ZooKeeper, leader election resets all session timeouts. All ephemeral znodes (locks) created by the previous leader's clients are orphans: no one is refreshing them. The new leader must re-establish sessions with all clients, and during this process, some leases may expire prematurely.

In etcd, the lease manager is tied to the Raft state machine. A leader change does not invalidate leases: the lease state is replicated across all nodes. However, during the election window (100-300ms), no lease renewals are processed. For leases with short TTLs (1-5 seconds), this can cause premature expiry.
====
A leader election in the lock service cluster pauses all lock operations. During the election window, no locks can be acquired or released, and lease renewals cannot be processed. Clients must decide what to do while they cannot confirm their locks.

**Root cause**: In ZooKeeper, sessions survive a leader election. Clients see a Disconnected event, reconnect to another server, and keep their session and their ephemeral znodes as long as they reconnect within the session timeout. The new leader restarts the session timers, so a healthy client is not penalized for the election. The risk appears when the election is slow or the client cannot reconnect in time: its session expires, its ephemeral znodes are deleted, and the lock is released while the client may still be running.

In etcd, lease state is part of the replicated state machine, so a leader change does not invalidate leases, and a new leader refreshes lease TTLs when it takes over. Renewals are not processed during the election window, which matters mainly for leases with very short TTLs (a second or two) compared with the election time.

In ZooKeeper, snapshots are taken by the leader. Follower nodes that restore from a leader's snapshot may be missing the latest transaction log entries. In etcd, `etcdctl snapshot restore` builds a new cluster from a snapshot file, and everything committed after that snapshot is gone (the routine internal snapshot, taken every 100,000 entries by default, is not the risk here, because the WAL is replayed on top of it). The restored cluster therefore has a clean but outdated view of lock state, and its revision counter, and any fencing token derived from it, is behind what clients have already seen.

In ZooKeeper, each server takes its own snapshots and replays its transaction log on startup. A single server that starts with old data is brought up to date by the leader, so the danger is restoring the whole ensemble from an old backup. If the snapshot is from 30 seconds ago and the TTL on all active locks is 60 seconds, the restored ensemble may show locks as held that their holders have long released, and it may miss locks that were granted afterwards.

### Failure Mode 8: Out-of-Order Message Delivery in Async Replication

When a lock service uses asynchronous replication (Redis master-replica, MySQL binary log replication), messages between the master and replica can arrive out of order. A lock release message may arrive at the replica before the corresponding lock acquisition message.

**Root cause**: Async replication uses a stream of log entries. If the network connection between master and replica has variable latency, or if the master queues entries and then sends them in bursts, the replica may apply updates in a different order than they occurred on the master. Worse, entries can be lost entirely if the master crashes before the replica has received all entries.
====
### Failure Mode 8: Lost Writes in Asynchronous Replication

When a lock service uses asynchronous replication (Redis master-replica, MySQL binary log replication), the replica is always some amount of time behind the master. A replica promoted after a master failure can be missing the most recent lock acquisitions and releases.

**Root cause**: Async replication ships a stream of log entries that the replica applies in order, but the master acknowledges each write to the client before the replica has it. If the master crashes before the replica has received all entries, the tail of the log is lost, and the promoted replica presents an older version of the lock state as if it were current.
