The probability of a split vote depends on the randomization spread and the number of nodes. With a spread of 150 ms (e.g., 150 to 300 ms), the probability that exactly one node times out first in the critical window is approximately 95% for a 5-node cluster. This drops to about 80% for a 9-node cluster, which is why larger clusters sometimes use wider randomization spreads.
====
The probability of a split vote depends on how wide the randomization spread is compared with the network round-trip time. If one node times out and its RequestVote messages reach the others before their own timers fire, that node wins cleanly. A wider spread makes a clean win more likely, and a narrower one makes split votes more likely, which is why the spread should be many times larger than the network round trip. If a split vote does happen, the cluster simply waits for the next randomized timeout and tries again, so elections still finish quickly in practice.

The lease also enables read-after-write consistency for followers. A follower can safely serve a read from its local state if the leader's lease has not expired, because no other node can be the leader during the lease period.
====
The lease also lets the leader serve linearizable reads from its own state without contacting the followers for each read: while its lease has not expired, no other node can have been elected. This depends on bounded clock drift between nodes. Followers should not serve such reads from their local state, because they may not yet have applied the latest committed writes.
