Answer: Two leaders existing simultaneously is a split-brain condition, which should not happen in a correct consensus implementation. The most likely causes are: a network partition split the cluster into two groups, each with a majority; or a bug in the leader election protocol. In Raft, a leader must receive a majority of votes to be elected. If the cluster is partitioned into two groups of 3 and 2 nodes, only the group of 3 can elect a leader (majority of 5). The group of 2 cannot elect a leader because it cannot reach majority. This is why odd-sized clusters are preferred.
====
Answer: In a correct implementation, two leaders can exist at the same moment only if they are in different terms: an old leader that has not yet noticed it was replaced still believes it is the leader, while a new leader has been elected in a higher term. Only the new-term leader can commit anything, because the old one cannot reach a majority, and the old leader steps down as soon as it hears the higher term. Two leaders in the same term should never happen: each node votes once per term, and a majority is required, so two candidates cannot both win. If you see it, suspect a bug or a misconfiguration (for example, nodes that do not persist their votes). If the cluster is partitioned into groups of 3 and 2 nodes out of 5, only the group of 3 can elect a leader, and an odd-sized cluster ensures that one side of any split has a majority.
