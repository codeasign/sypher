Atlassian ran Jira on a single PostgreSQL instance for over a decade. Discord scaled their Nitro membership service vertically before hitting limits and migrating to sharding. Cloudflare handles 25 million HTTP requests per second on single-node infrastructure for many of their services, pushing vertical scaling further than most companies attempt. These real-world stories reveal when vertical scaling works, where it breaks, and how to plan the migration.
====
Real systems rarely stay purely vertical or purely horizontal. Atlassian sized up its PostgreSQL instances and then partitioned tenants across many of them. Discord outgrew a single MongoDB replica set and moved to distributed stores. Cloudflare builds very capable individual servers and still runs many of them. Uber split one database by the characteristics of the data. These stories show when vertical scaling works, where it breaks, and how to plan the migration. The company sections describe the general shape of each story from their public engineering write-ups, and specific figures are illustrative unless stated.
