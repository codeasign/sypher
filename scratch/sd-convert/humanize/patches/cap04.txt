<<<<
Spanner (CP with TrueTime): Spanner uses TrueTime, Google's synchronized clock service, to provide external consistency without blocking during partitions. TrueTime gives Spanner a bounded uncertainty window on clock skew. During a partition, Spanner delays writes until clock uncertainty is resolved, rather than blocking entirely. This is closer to CP than AP, but with better partition behavior than traditional CP systems.
====
Spanner (CP): Spanner replicates each piece of data with Paxos, so during a partition the side that has a majority of a group's replicas keeps making progress, and the minority side cannot commit writes. Separately, Spanner uses TrueTime, Google's clock service with a bounded uncertainty, to provide externally consistent transactions: a transaction waits out the clock uncertainty (a few milliseconds) before it commits. That "commit wait" is about ordering, not about partitions. Spanner is technically CP. Google has argued that, because it runs on a private, highly redundant global network, partitions are rare enough that users experience it as almost always available.

Prevention: ZooKeeper's leader election typically completes in well under a few seconds. Raft uses randomized election timeouts to reduce the chance of concurrent elections. Clients should also reconnect with jittered backoff so they do not arrive all at once.

When the queue overflows, the oldest hints are discarded. The writes represented by those hints are lost. The system has silently lost data.
====
When the queue overflows, or when a node has been down longer than the hint window (Cassandra stops storing hints after 3 hours by default), hints are discarded. The writes represented by those hints are not replayed. The system has silently lost data on that replica until repair runs.

QUORUM provides the same strong consistency during normal operation but allows the system to survive node failures.
====
QUORUM reads and writes together still guarantee that reads see the latest write, but the system can survive a node failure.

Many systems that use CAP as an excuse are actually failing for other reasons: bad indexing, poor caching, inefficient queries. (Even without a partition, stronger consistency costs latency, which is the trade-off the PACELC model describes.)
