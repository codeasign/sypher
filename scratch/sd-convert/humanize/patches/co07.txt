**Read path optimization**: To reduce leader load, use read leases (also called leader leases). The leader grants a lease to followers: for a window of time (typically 1-2 seconds), the followers can serve linearizable reads directly without consulting the leader. The leader ensures no other leader can be elected during the lease period. This technique is used in etcd and Consul.
====
**Read path optimization**: To reduce leader load, the leader can serve linearizable reads from its own state while it holds a valid leader lease (during the lease, no other leader can be elected, assuming bounded clock drift), without a round of messages per read. To spread reads across followers, a follower can ask the leader for its current commit index and wait until it has applied up to that point before answering (the "read index" technique used by etcd), which is linearizable but still costs a small round trip to the leader. Some systems also grant time-based leases to replicas so they can serve reads locally.
