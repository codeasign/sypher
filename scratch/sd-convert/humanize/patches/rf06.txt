For multi-region writes, I would use CockroachDB's approach: each range has a leaseholder that serves reads and coordinates writes, and the system tries to keep the Raft leader on the same node. Ranges are placed so that the leaseholder sits in the region with the most traffic for that data, so different data can be "homed" in different regions. A write to a range still needs a majority of that range's replicas, so replicas are spread across regions according to the desired survival goal.

If an entire region goes offline, leadership and leases for the affected ranges move to replicas in the surviving regions, which can form majorities as long as the placement rules kept a majority of each range's replicas outside the failed region.
