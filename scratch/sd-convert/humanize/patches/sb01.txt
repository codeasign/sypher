Consensus algorithms like Paxos and Raft provide strong guarantees against split brain by ensuring that at most one leader can be elected per term (epoch). A deposed leader may briefly still believe it is leading, but it cannot get a majority to commit anything.

Witnesses are common in MongoDB replica sets and MySQL Group Replication. The witness adds no storage cost but introduces a single point of failure for the election process.
====
Witnesses appear in several products: MongoDB calls them arbiters (though their use is discouraged in modern deployments), and Windows failover clustering and SQL Server availability groups use witness resources. (MySQL Group Replication has no witness and needs three full members.) A witness adds almost no storage cost, but its availability matters: if the witness and one data node are lost together, quorum is lost.

Reality: Leaderless systems like Cassandra have no leader to split, but during a partition both sides can keep accepting writes for the same keys. The effect is the same kind of divergence, even though no leader election is involved.

Short lease durations cause false failovers. If the lease is shorter than the worst network or garbage collection delay, a healthy leader's lease expires, triggering an unnecessary election and failover. Long lease durations mean the cluster takes longer to detect a real leader failure and fail over. A 30-second lease means up to 30 seconds of downtime during a leader failure.

Not while remaining available. Two nodes can each believe the other is dead, and neither can tell a crashed peer from a partitioned one. You have two choices: continue independently (risking split brain) or require both nodes for every decision (safe, but any single failure stops the cluster). A witness or arbiter provides the third vote that lets one side continue safely. Some configurations use a shared disk or external lock service as a tiebreaker, but the principle is the same: a third decision entity is required. Clusters with an even number of nodes generally need a tiebreak rule for the same reason, because a perfect split leaves neither half with a majority.
