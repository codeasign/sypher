**Failure mode:** If the coordinator crashes after the prepare phase but before the commit phase, participants hold locks until a timeout (30-60 seconds by default). The coordinator recovers by reading its WAL and continuing the commit or abort.
====
**Failure mode:** If the coordinator crashes after the prepare phase but before the commit phase, the participants that voted "ready" are stuck in doubt. They cannot safely commit or abort on their own, so they keep holding their locks until the coordinator recovers, reads its WAL, and finishes the commit or abort (or until an operator resolves the in-doubt transactions by hand). This blocking behavior is the classic weakness of 2PC.

**`wal_buffers`**: Memory for WAL before fsync. Default: 16 MB. For write-heavy workloads: 64 MB. Larger buffers allow more WAL to accumulate before fsync, reducing write amplification but increasing potential data loss on crash.
====
**`wal_buffers`**: Memory for WAL before it is flushed. PostgreSQL sizes it automatically (up to 16 MB, one WAL segment), and values above 16 MB rarely help. Commits still wait for the WAL flush, so this setting does not change durability.

====
**`checkpoint_completion_target`**: Fraction of checkpoint interval to spread checkpoint writes. Default: 0.9 in PostgreSQL 14 and later (0.5 in older versions). A high value smooths out write spikes from checkpoints at the cost of slightly longer crash recovery.
