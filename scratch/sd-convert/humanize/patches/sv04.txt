Stateless services sit on the CA side of the CAP theorem: they prioritize Consistency and Availability, and they tolerate Partitions by failing when the database is unreachable. A stateless service that cannot reach its database returns errors. It does not serve inconsistent data.

Stateful services sit on the AP side: they prioritize Availability and Partition tolerance, and they accept Eventual Consistency. A stateful service that is partitioned continues to serve requests from local memory, even if that memory diverges from other nodes.
====
CAP describes the behavior of data stores, so a stateless service inherits its CAP position from the store it depends on. A stateless service in front of a strongly consistent database behaves like a CP system: when a partition cuts it off from the database, it returns errors rather than serving data that might be wrong. It never serves inconsistent data of its own, because it has none.

Stateful services can land on either side. A consensus-replicated service (Raft or Paxos) is CP: the minority side of a partition stops accepting writes. A service that keeps state in local memory and keeps serving during a partition is AP: it stays available, but its copies of the data can diverge and must be reconciled later.
