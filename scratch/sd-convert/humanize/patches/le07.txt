**Important caveats for a database:**
- The winner of the znode race is not necessarily the best standby. Before promoting, check that the standby has replayed the most WAL (the highest LSN), or you may lose committed writes.
- The old primary must be fenced (shut down or cut off from clients and storage) before the new one accepts writes. Otherwise a paused or partitioned old primary can keep accepting writes.
- A failed primary usually cannot rejoin as a standby without being rewound or rebuilt, because it may hold WAL the new primary never received (`pg_rewind` handles this for PostgreSQL).
- In practice, use a proven tool such as Patroni, which implements this pattern on top of etcd, ZooKeeper, or Consul.

**Solution 1: Basic Paxos with no distinguished leader.** In classic single-decree Paxos, any node can act as a proposer, so no election is required. However, competing proposers can keep invalidating each other's proposals (dueling proposers), so progress is not guaranteed. This is why practical Multi-Paxos implementations elect a distinguished proposer, which brings back a leader.
