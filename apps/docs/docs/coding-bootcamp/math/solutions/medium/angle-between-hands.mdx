# Angle Between Hands of a Clock — Solution

## Problem Overview

Given an hour (1-12) and minutes (0-59), find the smaller of the two angles formed between the clock's hour and minute hands.

## Brute Force Approach

There isn't really a "brute force" here — this is a pure geometry/arithmetic problem. The direct formula computation below **is** the optimal approach; there's no search or iteration to optimize away.

## Optimal Approach: Direct Angle Formula

**Idea:** Compute each hand's angle from the 12 o'clock position independently, take the absolute difference, then fold that difference around the circle if it's more than half the clock face.

**Algorithm:**
1. The minute hand travels 360° over 60 minutes: `minute_angle = minutes * 6`.
2. The hour hand travels 360° over 12 hours (30° per hour), *plus* it creeps forward within the current hour as minutes pass: `hour_angle = (hour % 12) * 30 + minutes * 0.5`.
3. `diff = abs(hour_angle - minute_angle)`.
4. The two hands actually form two angles that sum to 360° (one going clockwise, one counterclockwise) — return the smaller one: `min(diff, 360 - diff)`.

**Why This Works:** Both hands move at a constant angular speed, so their positions at any given `(hour, minutes)` are fully determined by simple multiplication — no simulation needed. The `hour % 12` handles the `hour = 12` edge case (which should behave identically to `hour = 0` on a 12-hour face), and the final `min(...)` step accounts for the fact that "the angle between two rays" is ambiguous between two supplementary values unless you explicitly pick the smaller one.

**Complexity Analysis:**
- **Time:** O(1) — fixed number of arithmetic operations.
- **Space:** O(1).

## Deep Dive

### Visual Walkthrough

For `hour = 12, minutes = 30`:

```
minute_angle = 30 * 6 = 180        (minute hand points at the 6)
hour_angle   = (12 % 12) * 30 + 30 * 0.5
             = 0 + 15
             = 15                  (hour hand has crept a quarter-hour past 12)
diff         = |180 - 15| = 165
answer       = min(165, 360 - 165) = min(165, 195) = 165
```

### Edge Cases to Consider

- **`hour = 12`**: must be treated as `0` for the hour-angle formula — `12 % 12 == 0` handles this automatically as long as you remember the modulo.
- **The two hands overlap**: e.g. `hour = 12, minutes = 0` gives `diff = 0`.
- **The two hands are opposite**: e.g. `hour = 6, minutes = 0` gives `diff = 180`, and `min(180, 180) = 180` — both halves are equal, which is correct.
- **`diff` alone is more than 180°**: this is exactly when the `360 - diff` branch is the real answer — e.g. `hour = 12, minutes = 45` gives a raw `diff` of `247.5`, but the actual (smaller) angle is `112.5`.
- **Half-degree results**: any time `minutes` is odd, `minutes * 0.5` is a `.5` value, so the final answer can legitimately end in `.5` rather than `.0` — this isn't a rounding artifact, it's exact.

## Key Takeaways

- Both clock hands move at constant angular speed, so their positions are pure arithmetic — no simulation or search needed.
- `hour % 12` is the whole trick to handling `hour = 12` correctly — forgetting it is the most common mistake on this problem.
- The two hands always form two angles that sum to 360°; always return `min(diff, 360 - diff)`, not just the raw difference.
- Results can legitimately be `.5` values, not just whole numbers — this comes from `minutes * 0.5` in the hour-hand formula whenever `minutes` is odd.
