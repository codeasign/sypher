# Min Cost Climbing Stairs — Solution

## Problem Overview

Given the cost of standing on each stair, find the minimum total cost to reach the top (one step past the last stair), where each move advances by one or two steps and you may start from step `0` or step `1`.

## Brute Force Approach

**Idea:** Recursively try both choices (advance one step or two) from every position, exploring all possible paths to the top and taking the cheapest.

**Complexity Analysis:**
- **Time:** O(2^n) — two branching choices at every step, without memoization.
- **Space:** O(n) — recursion depth.

## Optimal Approach: Bottom-Up Dynamic Programming

**Idea:** Define `dp[i]` as the minimum total cost *already paid* by the time you leave step `i` (i.e., including `cost[i]` itself). Since you can only arrive at step `i` from step `i-1` or step `i-2`, `dp[i]` is just `cost[i]` plus whichever of those two was cheaper to leave. Build this up from the bottom, then the final answer is the cheaper of the two ways to make the *last* jump to the top — from the last step or the second-to-last step.

**Algorithm:**
1. Base cases: `dp[0] = cost[0]`, `dp[1] = cost[1]` (starting on either step 0 or step 1 is allowed for free — the only cost incurred is the step's own value).
2. For each `i` from `2` to `n-1`: `dp[i] = cost[i] + min(dp[i-1], dp[i-2])`.
3. The top is one step past the last index, reachable from either `n-1` or `n-2` — return `min(dp[n-1], dp[n-2])`.

**Why This Works:** Every path to step `i` must have come from step `i-1` or step `i-2`, and since we want the *minimum* total cost, the best way to be standing on step `i` is always to have arrived via whichever of those two predecessors was cheapest — later choices can't retroactively make an earlier suboptimal choice better. This is the classic optimal-substructure property that makes dynamic programming applicable.

**Complexity Analysis:**
- **Time:** O(n) — one pass, computing each `dp[i]` once.
- **Space:** O(n) for the full `dp` array, reducible to O(1) since only the previous two values are ever needed.

## Deep Dive

### Visual Walkthrough

For `cost = [10, 15, 20]`:

```
dp[0] = 10                      (cost of standing on step 0)
dp[1] = 15                      (cost of standing on step 1)
dp[2] = cost[2] + min(dp[1], dp[0]) = 20 + min(15, 10) = 20 + 10 = 30

answer = min(dp[2], dp[1]) = min(30, 15) = 15
```

The optimal path: start at step 1 (free to start there), pay `15`, then jump *two* steps straight to the top — never even touching step 2's cost of `20`.

### Edge Cases to Consider

- **Only two steps** (`n = 2`, the minimum per constraints): `dp[0]` and `dp[1]` are the only values computed; the answer is `min(dp[1], dp[0])` directly — the cheaper of the two starting steps, since either one is a single jump from the top.
- **All zero costs**: every `dp[i]` stays `0`, so the answer is `0` regardless of path length.
- **A cheap step surrounded by expensive ones** (e.g. `[100, 1, 1, 100]`): the DP naturally routes through the cheap steps, since `dp[i-1]`/`dp[i-2]` always reflects the truly cheapest way to have arrived, not just the most recent step.
- **Alternating cheap/expensive costs**: the recurrence doesn't need to "look ahead" to know which pattern is optimal — taking the min of the two predecessors at each step correctly threads through the cheapest steps as it goes.

def min_cost_climbing_stairs(cost: list) -> int:
    n = len(cost)
    prev2, prev1 = cost[0], cost[1]
    for i in range(2, n):
        curr = cost[i] + min(prev1, prev2)
        prev2, prev1 = prev1, curr
    return min(prev1, prev2)

## Key Takeaways

- `dp[i]` represents the cost of *leaving* step `i`, not just standing on it — this framing makes the final answer a clean `min` over the last two positions, since the "top" isn't itself a cost-bearing step.
- The recurrence only ever needs the previous two values, so the space can always be reduced from a full O(n) array down to two rolling variables — a very common DP optimization once the recurrence only references a fixed-size window of prior state.
- Being allowed to start on either step `0` or step `1` for free is exactly why both `dp[0]` and `dp[1]` are initialized directly from `cost`, rather than one of them being derived from the other.
