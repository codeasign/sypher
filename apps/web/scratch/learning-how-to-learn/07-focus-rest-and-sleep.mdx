You read the same paragraph three times. Your eyes move, but nothing goes in. You look at your phone, then back, and read it a fourth time.

The problem is not the paragraph. It is your state. Learning depends on attention, and attention depends on rest, so how you treat your brain matters as much as the method.

## Two ways of thinking

When you concentrate hard on a problem, you are in a focused mode. When you relax, walk, or let your mind wander, you are in a more relaxed mode. Both are useful. Focused thinking follows a known path. Relaxed thinking lets your brain make broader connections in the background, which is why ideas often arrive in the shower or on a walk.

If you are stuck on a hard problem, working harder for another hour often does less than a break.

## Work in short blocks

Many people concentrate best in periods of 25 to 50 minutes, followed by a break of five to ten minutes. During the break, stand up, drink water, or look out of the window. Avoid your phone, which keeps your attention busy.

## Protect your focus

Put your phone in another room. Close extra tabs. Tell the people around you that you are studying for an hour. A single interruption can cost far more time than it takes.

## Sleep is part of studying

During sleep, the brain sorts and strengthens what you learned during the day. Studying late and skipping sleep usually loses more than it gains. A good night's sleep after learning, and before a test, helps a great deal.

Bad version: "I will stay up until three and learn everything."

Better version: "I will study until eleven, sleep, and do a quick recall test in the morning."

What makes it better: your memory of the day gets processed overnight, and you wake up sharper.

## Move your body

A short walk or some exercise can raise your energy and mood. You do not need a hard workout. A twenty minute walk helps many people focus.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. Plan a two hour study session with breaks.

**Suggested answer:** "Forty minutes of study, ten minute walk, forty minutes of study, ten minutes of rest, then twenty minutes of recall."

Exercise 2. You feel stuck on a problem after an hour. Write what you do.

**Suggested answer:** "Take a twenty minute walk with no phone, then return and re-read the question with fresh eyes."

Exercise 3. Write one change to protect your sleep before an exam week.

**Suggested answer:** "Set a fixed bedtime of 11 and switch off screens at 10:30."
