Two students prepare for the same exam. One reads the textbook three times, highlights half of every page, and feels ready. The other reads it once, closes the book, and tries to write down everything she can recall. She feels terrible after the first attempt because she can only remember a third of it.

On exam day, the second student does better. Nobody is surprised except the first student.

This course is about why that happens. Most of us were never taught how to learn. We were taught what to learn, and left to guess the method. The guess usually turns out to be rereading and highlighting, which feel productive and work poorly.

The methods that work are well studied, and they are not complicated. They ask you to spend your effort differently: less time taking information in, more time pulling it back out, spacing your practice, and getting feedback.

What you will learn:

* Why common study habits create a false sense of knowing
* How to set a clear goal and map a subject before you dive into details
* How to test yourself instead of rereading
* How to space your practice and mix topics
* How to explain an idea simply, and why that exposes gaps
* How focus, breaks, and sleep affect what sticks
* How to practice with feedback and learn from mistakes
* How to take notes that help you think
* How to build a routine that keeps you learning

Each lesson is short and ends with a TRY IT of two or three exercises. Use your own subject as you go: a language, a professional skill, a certification, or a hobby. The exercises work on anything.

You do not need to be naturally quick. Learning well is a set of habits, and habits can be built at any age.
