---
title: "Practice with feedback and learn from mistakes"
order: 8
---

A man plays tennis every weekend for twenty years and never gets better. Another takes ten lessons and improves within months. The difference is not the hours. It is what happens inside them.

Repeating what you already do well feels good but builds little. Getting better takes practice that stretches you a little, with feedback on what went wrong.

## Aim at the edge of your ability

Work on things that are a bit too hard, so that you fail some of the time but succeed on others. If you never fail, you are too comfortable. If you always fail, the task is too big, so break it into a smaller piece.

## Break skills into parts

Decide which small part is weakest. A singer may practice only the high note in the chorus. A writer may work on openings for a week. Focus on one part at a time, and put it back into the whole later.

## Get feedback quickly

Feedback is information about the gap between what you did and what you were aiming for. The sooner you get it, the better. Ask a teacher, a coach, or a colleague who is good at it. Or check your work against a model answer. Record yourself speaking or playing, and listen with a critical ear.

Bad version: "I practiced for two hours, so I must be improving."

Better version: "I practiced the difficult passage for 20 minutes, recorded it, and heard that my timing rushes at bar 8. Tomorrow I will slow that bar down."

This is better because you know what to fix, so the next session has a target.

## Treat mistakes as data

A mistake tells you what you have not learned yet. Keep a list of your errors, and read it before the next session. Patterns show up, such as always forgetting the same rule.

## Ask for the kind of feedback you need

Bad request: "What do you think?" Better request: "Was my explanation clear in the second section? Where did you get lost?"

## Be patient with plateaus

Progress is often uneven. You may stay at the same level for weeks and then jump. Keep practicing with purpose during the flat parts.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. You are learning to present. Name one small part to practice.

**Suggested answer:** "The first 30 seconds. I will practice my opening five times and record it."

Exercise 2. Write a request for useful feedback on a report you wrote.

**Suggested answer:** "Could you look at page two? I would like to know if the recommendation is clear, and where you had to reread."

Exercise 3. Turn a mistake into a rule.

**Suggested answer:** "I keep mixing up 'affect' and 'effect'. Rule: affect is usually the verb, effect is usually the noun. I will check it before I send any email."
