"Can you explain that in plain words?"

It is a request that scares us, because it reveals whether we understand something or only know its vocabulary. That is exactly why it is a powerful way to learn.

## The plain-language method

Take an idea you are studying. Explain it as if to a smart friend who knows nothing about the subject. Use short words and everyday examples. Speak it or write it. When you get stuck, or find yourself using jargon to cover a hole, you have found a gap. Go back to your source, fix the gap, and explain again.

This is often called the Feynman technique, after a physicist known for explaining hard ideas simply.

## An example

A student studying inflation writes: "Inflation is a general increase in the price level and a decline in purchasing power." Nice sentence. She tries again in plain words: "Inflation means that the same amount of money buys less than it used to. If a coffee costs 3 today and 3.30 next year, and your pay did not change, you are a bit poorer."

The second version is what she understands. The first is what she memorized.

## Use analogies

Connect the new idea to something you already know. A computer's memory is like a desk, and its storage is like a filing cabinet. The comparison will not be perfect, but it gives you a foothold.

Bad version: "I know what it means, I just cannot put it into words."

Better version: "If I cannot put it into words, I do not know it well enough yet. I will look again at the part I keep skipping."

The gain here: you use the struggle as information, not as evidence that you are not clever.

## Teach someone

Explaining to a real person is even better. Friends, family, or a study partner will ask questions you did not expect. If nobody is available, write a short blog post or a note to your future self.

## Keep it short

Aim for a two minute explanation. If it takes ten, you have not yet found the core.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. Explain "interest on a loan" in two sentences a child could follow.

**Suggested answer:** "When you borrow money, you pay a little extra for using it. That extra is called interest, and it is the price of borrowing."

Exercise 2. You cannot explain a term without using the term itself. Write what you do next.

**Suggested answer:** "I look it up in a beginner source, find one example, and try again with different words."

Exercise 3. Write an analogy for "a database index".

**Suggested answer:** "It is like the index at the back of a book. Instead of reading every page to find a topic, you look it up and go straight to the page."
