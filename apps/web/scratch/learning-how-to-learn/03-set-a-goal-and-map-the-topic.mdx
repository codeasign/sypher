---
title: "Set a goal and map the topic"
order: 3
---

Starting to learn a big subject can feel like walking into a huge library with no signs. You pick a shelf at random, read for an hour, and later realize you spent it on a detail nobody needs.

A little planning at the start saves a great deal of wandering.

## Say what you want to be able to do

Learning goals work best when they describe an action. "Learn Spanish" is vague. "Hold a ten minute conversation about my day, with pauses" is something you can practice and test.

Bad version: "I want to learn data analysis."

Better version: "In three months, I want to take a messy spreadsheet, clean it, and produce three charts that answer a question for my manager."

This is better because you know what to practice, and you will know when you have arrived.

## Get the big picture first

Before the details, spend an hour finding the shape of the subject. Read a contents page, a short overview, or a beginner article. Write down the main parts and how they connect. This can be a list or a rough drawing with boxes and arrows. It does not need to be neat.

Now every new fact has a place to go. Learning is easier when new information attaches to something already there.

## Find the core

Most subjects have a small core that gives most of the benefit. In a language, a few hundred common words cover a large share of everyday speech. In a software tool, a handful of features do most of the work. Ask a person who is good at it, "What are the five things a beginner must know first?"

## Choose your sources

Pick one or two good sources and stay with them for a while. Jumping between ten courses feels productive, but you keep repeating the first chapter.

## Set a small starting target

Plan a first week that is easy to complete. Momentum matters more than ambition at the beginning.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. Turn "learn to cook" into a goal with an action and a test.

**Suggested answer:** "By the end of the month, I can cook five simple dinners without a recipe open, and one of them for guests."

Exercise 2. You want to learn a new subject. Write the question you would ask an expert.

**Suggested answer:** "If you were starting again, what would you learn first, and what would you skip for now?"

Exercise 3. Sketch a map for a subject you are learning, with at least four parts.

**Suggested answer:** Example for personal finance: "Income, spending, saving, debt, investing, insurance. Arrows show that spending and income decide how much can go to saving."
