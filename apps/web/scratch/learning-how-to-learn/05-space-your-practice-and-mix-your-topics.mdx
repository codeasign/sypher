title: "Space your practice and mix your topics"

Cramming works, in a narrow sense. If you study all night before a test, you will probably pass. A week later, most of it is gone.

If you want knowledge that stays, there are two ways to arrange your time that make a large difference: spacing and mixing.

## Spacing

Spacing means spreading the same amount of study over several days instead of one long session. Every time you return to something you have started to forget, the effort of remembering strengthens it. A common pattern is to review after a day, then three days, then a week, then a month.

Three sessions of 40 minutes across a week usually beat one session of two hours, even though the total time is the same.

Bad version: "Saturday: study everything for five hours."

Better version: "Monday 40 minutes, Wednesday 40 minutes, Friday 40 minutes, Sunday a 20 minute recall test."

It works because the forgetting between sessions creates the effort that builds memory.

## Mixing, also called interleaving

Instead of practicing one type of problem 20 times in a row, mix different types in one session. A guitar player might play scales, a chord change, and a song in turn. A math student might mix fractions, percentages, and ratios.

It feels slower and less smooth. But you practice choosing the right method, not just repeating one, and that is what you need in real life.

## Do not fear the feeling of difficulty

Blocked practice, one topic at a time, makes you feel good because you improve quickly within the session. Spaced and mixed practice feels messy. The messiness is the signal that your brain is working on it.

## Plan it simply

You do not need software. Put review dates in your calendar right after you learn something new. A note that says "Thursday: test yourself on chapter 3" is enough.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. You need to learn 30 new words. Write a spacing plan for two weeks.

**Suggested answer:** "Learn 10 words a day for three days. Test all 30 on day 4, day 7, and day 14. Any word I miss gets tested again the next day."

Exercise 2. Write a mixed practice session for learning to cook.

**Suggested answer:** "Twenty minutes each: chopping vegetables, making a sauce, timing rice and eggs. Rotate them rather than doing one for an hour."

Exercise 3. Choose the better plan for a 6 hour weekend study: one block or three. Explain in a sentence.

**Suggested answer:** "Three blocks with breaks and a recall test at the end, since spaced and tested practice keeps more than one long session."
