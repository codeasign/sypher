title: "Come up with options before you judge them"

Try this for one minute: write down every way you could get to work tomorrow. Do not judge them, just write.

Most people list three or four, all versions of the same route. If you keep going, you will get to odd ones, like borrowing a bike, leaving an hour earlier to avoid traffic, working from home, or sharing a ride. One of those odd ones might be the best.

## Why we stop too early

When a problem appears, the mind grabs the first workable answer and starts defending it. That first answer is often fine, but you cannot know that until you have seen a few others.

## Two rules for a good list

Quantity first. Aim for at least six options, even if some feel silly. The first three are usually the obvious ones.

No judging yet. If you say "that will not work" while listing, the list dries up. Judge in a separate step.

## Use prompts to stretch the list

* What if we had no budget? What if we had unlimited budget?
* What if we did the opposite?
* What would a friend from a different job do?
* What is the smallest version of this?
* Could we skip the problem instead of solving it?

Bad version: "We have to hire another support person. There is no other way."

Better version: "Options: hire someone, add a help page for the top five questions, offer a call-back form, move the busiest hour to a chat bot, close support on Sundays and tell customers, share the load with sales."

It works because it puts real choices on the table. Hiring may still win, but now you know why.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. Your meetings run long. List five options, including at least one unusual one.

**Suggested answer:** "Set a 25 minute limit, send an agenda in advance, hold meetings standing, cancel the weekly one and use a shared note, require a decision at the end." Any list that includes different kinds of ideas is good.

Exercise 2. You cannot afford a big trip. List four ways to get a break that does not cost much.

**Suggested answer:** "Take a day trip, swap houses with a friend for a weekend, plan a day with no screens, visit a nearby town by train."

Exercise 3. Take your first idea for any current problem. Write two more before choosing.

**Suggested answer:** For example, if your first idea is "hire a helper", the next two might be "ask the supplier to deliver in the morning" and "stop offering the slow service on busy days". They differ in kind, not just in detail.
