Kavya's laptop battery dies by noon, so she buys a bigger charger. It still dies by noon. She buys a new battery. Same result. It takes a colleague, glancing at her screen, to notice she has forty browser tabs playing video in the background.

She fixed the symptom three times. The cause was sitting in plain sight.

## Ask why, then ask again

The simplest tool is to ask "why?" until you reach something you can change. It is often called the five whys, though the number is not important.

The website went down. Why? The server ran out of memory. Why? A report was running that loaded every record. Why? Someone scheduled it for the middle of the day. Why? The scheduling tool defaults to noon. Now you have a real fix: change the default, and warn people about heavy reports.

Compare that with the first answer, "restart the server." That works today and fails next week.

## Stop when you can act

You do not need exactly five steps. Stop when the answer is something a person can change. If the answer is "because people are careless", you went too far and the answer is not useful. Go back one step and ask, "what made carelessness easy?"

## Look for what changed

Many problems start after a change. Ask, "When did this begin, and what was different then?" A new version, a new person, a new rule, a new supplier. This question alone solves a surprising number of problems.

Bad version: "This keeps happening, so I guess it is just how things are."

Better version: "It started three weeks ago, right after we switched suppliers. Let me compare the old and new parts."

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. Customers keep complaining that deliveries arrive late. Write three "why" steps.

**Suggested answer:** "Why late? Orders are packed after the courier's pickup time. Why? Packing starts at 3 PM, but pickup is at 2:30. Why? The order cut-off is 3 PM, set years ago when pickup was later." The fix is to align the cut-off with the pickup.

Exercise 2. A child forgets their homework every Monday. Name one "what changed" question.

**Suggested answer:** "Is Monday different because the bag is packed on the weekend instead of Sunday night?"

Exercise 3. Your team keeps missing deadlines. Which answer is too far: "we lack discipline" or "estimates are made before the requirements are clear"?

**Suggested answer:** "We lack discipline" is too far. It blames people and gives you nothing to change. The second names a process you can fix.
