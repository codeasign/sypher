---
title: "Define the real problem"
order: 2
---

Most problems are solved badly because they were described badly. If you name the wrong problem, even a perfect solution helps nobody.

Here is a typical example. A team lead says, "We need a faster way to approve invoices." The team spends six weeks building an approval tool. When it launches, invoices are still slow. The delay was never the approval step. It was that invoices sat in someone's email for four days before anyone looked at them.

## Ask what is actually wrong

A good problem statement says what is happening, how you know, and what you want instead. It does not contain a solution.

Bad version: "We need a better filing system."

Better version: "Two times last month we could not find a signed contract when a client asked for it. It took a day each time. I want to find any contract in under five minutes."

The second version works because it has evidence and a target. It also leaves room for many solutions, and a "better filing system" is only one of them.

## Watch for solutions disguised as problems

If your problem statement starts with "we need to buy", "we should hire", or "we have to build", you have probably jumped ahead. Try changing it to "what is going wrong that made us think of this?"

## Check it with one more person

Say your problem statement out loud to someone who was not there. If they ask, "Wait, why is that a problem?", the statement is not clear yet. That is useful to learn before you spend money.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. Rewrite this as a real problem: "We need a new project management app."

**Suggested answer:** "Tasks keep getting dropped between the design and development teams. Last quarter, four requests were never started because nobody knew they had been handed over. I want every request to have one visible owner." This describes what is wrong without naming the tool.

Exercise 2. Rewrite: "I need to wake up earlier."

**Suggested answer:** "I am tired and rushed every morning, and I miss the bus twice a week. I want to leave home at 8:00 without hurrying." Now waking earlier is one option among several, such as preparing the night before.

Exercise 3. Write a problem statement for something bothering you this week, with one piece of evidence and one target.

**Suggested answer:** For example: "I spend about two hours a day answering messages and get little else done. I want two clear hours for focused work."
