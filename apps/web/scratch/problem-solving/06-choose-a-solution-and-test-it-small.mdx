title: "Choose a solution and test it small"

"We will find out after launch."

That sentence has cost companies a great deal of money. A better one is, "We will find out by Friday, with ten customers."

Once you have options, you need to choose. Most people either agonize forever or pick fast and commit fully. A steadier approach is to choose the most promising option and test a small version of it first.

## Pick with three questions

For each option ask:

* How much will it help?
* How hard is it, in time, money, and effort?
* What is the risk if it fails?

You will often find one option that helps a lot, is easy, and is safe to try. Start there.

## Make the test small and clear

A test needs a question, a short time, and a result you can read.

Bad version: "Let us roll out the new schedule to the whole company and see how it goes."

Better version: "Let us try the new schedule with the warehouse team for two weeks. We will compare late orders before and after. If late orders drop by a fifth, we roll it out."

This is better because you decided in advance what success looks like. Without that, everyone reads the results the way they already wanted to.

## Plan for failure

Ask, "If this does not work, what will we do?" Write down the way back. A test you can undo is much easier to say yes to.

## Leave room for a good enough answer

Some problems do not need the best solution. If the printer jam costs five minutes a week, do not spend a day finding a perfect fix. Match the effort to the size of the problem.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. You want to know if a new email subject line gets more opens. Design a small test.

**Suggested answer:** "Send version A to half the list and version B to the other half on the same day. Compare open rates after 24 hours."

Exercise 2. Write a success condition for trying a new morning routine for one week.

**Suggested answer:** "I leave the house by 8:00 on at least four of five days without skipping breakfast."

Exercise 3. Your team wants to switch to a new tool. Write the "way back".

**Suggested answer:** "We keep the old tool running for a month and export data weekly, so we can switch back without losing anything."
