title: "Get unstuck"
order: 7
---

The cursor blinks on a blank page. Forty minutes have gone by, and the only thing that changed is that you have checked your phone twice.

Being stuck feels like a personal failing. It is not. It is what happens when you try to think of an answer you do not yet have the pieces for, and it happens to experienced people all the time.

## Change the question

If "how do I write this report?" gets you nowhere, try a smaller one: "What is the one sentence I want the reader to remember?" Write that. The report often follows.

## Explain it to someone else

Say the problem out loud, from the beginning, to a colleague, a friend, or even a rubber duck on your desk. Many times you will hear yourself say the answer halfway through. The act of explaining forces you to put fuzzy thoughts into order.

## Work backward

Start from the finished result and ask what had to be true just before it. Then what had to be true before that? This is good for planning, such as a launch, a trip, or an exam schedule.

## Take a real break

Stepping away is not laziness. A ten minute walk, or sleeping on it, lets the mind keep working without pushing. Bad version: "I will sit here until I solve it." Better version: "I have tried for 45 minutes. I will take a walk, and come back with a fresh page."

## Lower the bar for a first attempt

Give yourself permission to write a bad first version. It is easier to fix a poor draft than to invent a perfect one.

## Ask the right kind of help

Bad version: "It does not work, can you help?"

Better version: "I am trying to send the invoice from the new system. I clicked Send, and it shows an error that says 'missing tax code'. I have checked that the field is filled in. What else could I look at?"

That change matters because it shows what you tried, so the other person can start where you stopped.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. You cannot decide how to start a presentation. Write a smaller question to get moving.

**Suggested answer:** "What is the one thing I want them to do after the talk?"

Exercise 2. Write a request for help that shows what you have already tried.

**Suggested answer:** "The report total does not match the source data. I have checked the date filter and removed duplicates. Could you check whether the formula is right?"

Exercise 3. Plan backward: you want to host a dinner on Saturday at 7 PM. What must be true at 6 PM?

**Suggested answer:** "The food is cooked or nearly ready, the table is set, and I am dressed. At 3 PM, shopping must be finished."
