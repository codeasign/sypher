Four people are in a room. Everyone is talking, and each person is describing a different problem. The meeting ends with a list of ten action items and no agreement on what went wrong.

Group problem solving fails in a predictable way: people start with solutions before they agree on the problem. The steps you learned already still apply. You just need to run them together.

## Start by agreeing on the problem

Take two minutes at the beginning. Ask, "Can we each say in one sentence what we think the problem is?" Write the answers on a board. You will often find two or three different problems hiding in one room.

## Separate ideas from judging

Use the same rule as before. First collect ideas with no comments, then discuss. Give quiet people a chance by asking everyone to write ideas alone for three minutes before sharing.

## Keep it about the problem, not the person

Bad version: "This happened because Dan was late with his part."

Better version: "The handover happened a day late. What in our process made that easy to miss?"

What makes it better: people stay open. If someone feels blamed, they defend themselves and stop giving real information.

## Decide how you will decide

Before you pick a solution, say how: by consensus, by vote, or by one person after hearing everyone. Without this, groups drift, or the loudest voice wins.

## End with owners and dates

Write down each action, who owns it, and when you will check. "Someone should look into that" means nobody will.

## When you disagree

Ask, "What would need to be true for your option to be the best one?" This turns a fight into a shared investigation.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. Your team argues about whether to hire or automate. Write a sentence to help the group agree on the problem first.

**Suggested answer:** "Before we choose, can we agree on what is going wrong? Is it that we are too slow, or that mistakes are getting through?"

Exercise 2. Someone says, "Sales keeps making promises we cannot keep." Rewrite it as a problem about the process.

**Suggested answer:** "Customers are being promised delivery dates before we have confirmed them. What would help sales check availability first?"

Exercise 3. End a meeting with a clear action.

**Suggested answer:** "Priya will list the top five complaints by Friday. Omar will book a call with the supplier by Thursday. We check in on Monday."
