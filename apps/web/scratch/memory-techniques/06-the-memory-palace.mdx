Walk through your front door in your mind. What is the first thing you see? Perhaps a mat, a coat rack, a lamp on a shelf.

You just used a place you know well as storage. That is the idea behind the memory palace, also called the method of loci. It is one of the oldest and strongest memory tools, used by speakers in ancient Rome and by competition champions today.

## How it works

Choose a place you know very well, such as your home, your route to work, or a park. Pick a fixed path through it, with five to ten specific spots. Then place one memory picture at each spot. To recall, you walk the path in your mind and see each picture.

## Building your first one

Take your home. The spots could be: front door, coat rack, sofa, television, kitchen table, fridge, bedroom door, bed.

Suppose you want to remember a talk with five points: goals, budget, schedule, risks, next steps. At the front door, picture a huge soccer goal blocking the entrance (goals). On the coat rack, hang bags of money (budget). On the sofa, a giant calendar sits reading the newspaper (schedule).

## Keep the rules simple

* Always walk in the same direction.
* Use one item per spot.
* Use vivid, funny pictures, as in the last lesson.
* Do not reuse the same place for a different list until you have cleared the old images. Imagine a cleaner walking through and sweeping them out.

Bad version: "I will just remember the five points."

Better version: "I walk the path and see the goal, the money, the calendar."

What makes it better: your brain already has a strong map of the place, so you need only to attach the new item.

## Where it is useful

Speeches without notes, lists, the order of steps in a task, or a set of facts for an exam. It is less useful for detailed, connected ideas, where understanding matters more than order.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. Name eight spots in your home that you could use as a path.

**Suggested answer:** "Front door, shoe rack, hallway mirror, sofa, table, kitchen sink, fridge, bedroom door." Any fixed order works.

Exercise 2. Use the first three spots to store: passport, charger, sunglasses.

**Suggested answer:** "A huge passport blocks the front door. The shoe rack is charging like a phone. The hallway mirror wears sunglasses."

Exercise 3. Walk the path and write the items without looking back.

**Suggested answer:** "Passport, charger, sunglasses." If one is missing, make the picture stranger.
