title: "Pay attention first"
order: 3
---

"Sorry, what was your name again?"

You asked it because you were thinking about what you would say next while the person was speaking. That is the real reason we forget most things. We never noticed them properly.

Attention is the entry gate of memory. No technique helps if the information does not get in.

## Do one thing at a time

Memory drops when you split attention. If you are on your phone while listening to instructions, you will catch parts. Put the phone down for the ten seconds that matter, and you will remember much more.

## Say it back

When someone gives you a detail, repeat it: "Room 214, at three." Saying it uses your ears and mouth, and it also confirms you heard it correctly. It is the cheapest memory trick there is.

## Make a deliberate moment

For things that matter, use a short ritual: stop, look at it, and say to yourself, "This is what I need to remember." It sounds silly, but it moves the fact from background noise to a marked item.

Bad version: "I put my passport somewhere safe." (You did not really see where.)

Better version: "I am putting my passport in the top drawer, on the left, under the blue folder." (You said it, and you saw it.)

It works because you paid a few seconds of attention, and it saves ten minutes of searching.

## Reduce interruptions

Silence notifications when you study or attend a meeting. Every interruption forces your brain to reload, and small details get lost each time.

## Notice what you care about

You remember things that matter to you. Ask yourself why a fact matters to you, even a small reason. It gives your brain a hook.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. Your manager gives you three instructions in a hallway. Write what you say back.

**Suggested answer:** "So, send the report to Lena by noon, book the meeting room for Thursday, and update the sheet. Is that right?"

Exercise 2. Write a sentence you can say aloud when you park in a big car park.

**Suggested answer:** "Level three, near the blue stairs, row F."

Exercise 3. Choose one thing you often forget. Write a small ritual to mark it.

**Suggested answer:** "When I lock the door, I stop, touch the handle, and say, 'Locked.'"
