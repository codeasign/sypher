---
title: "Review at the right times"
order: 8
---

You read a chapter on Sunday. You feel fine. By Wednesday, half of it is gone. That is normal, and it can be fixed with less work than you might think.

The key is that you review just as you are about to forget, not before, and not long after.

## Active recall

Testing yourself beats rereading. Close the book and ask, "What were the main points?" Try to write or say them. Then check. The effort of pulling it out makes the memory stronger, even when you get things wrong.

Bad version: "I read my notes three times."

Better version: "I read my notes once, then closed them and wrote down everything I remembered. I checked what I missed."

It works because it uses recall, which is the actual skill you need on the day.

## Spaced repetition

Instead of one long session, spread short reviews over time. A common pattern: review after one day, then three days, then a week, then a month. Each time, the fact lasts longer before it fades.

A simple approach is to review new material:

* The same day, briefly
* The next day
* After three or four days
* After a week
* After a month

Each review can be five minutes.

## Flashcards, done well

Write a question on one side and the answer on the other. Keep each card to one idea. If you got a card right, put it aside for longer. If you got it wrong, see it again soon. Apps do this automatically, but paper works fine.

## Mix topics

Instead of studying one subject for three hours, alternate subjects. It feels harder and works better.

## Explain it in your own words

If you can explain an idea to a friend without notes, you know it. Where you stumble is where you need review.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. You learned ten new words in a language today. Write a review plan for the next two weeks.

**Suggested answer:** "Test myself tomorrow, then in three days, a week, and two weeks. Words I miss return to the next day."

Exercise 2. Turn this fact into a flashcard: "The capital of Australia is Canberra."

**Suggested answer:** "Front: Capital of Australia? Back: Canberra."

Exercise 3. Write how you would test yourself on a meeting you just attended.

**Suggested answer:** "Close my notes and list the three decisions and the owners. Then check what I missed."
