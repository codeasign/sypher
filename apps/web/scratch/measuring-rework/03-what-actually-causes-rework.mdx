Cascade tagged every follow-up fix with a root cause. Across the 10 sprints: 46 hours traced to unclear requirements, 28 to something missed in review, 19 to scope changing after work started, and 12 to a flaky test hiding a real failure.

| Cause | Hours | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unclear requirements | 46 | 43.8% |
| Missed in review | 28 | 26.7% |
| Late scope change | 19 | 18.1% |
| Flaky test masked it | 12 | 11.4% |

![Bar chart comparing rework hours by root cause across four categories](https://syhpher-next-datastore-gvaf.b-cdn.net/svgs/measuring-rework/rework-by-cause.svg)

*Shown as a bar chart because the point is comparing four causes by how many hours each one costs, which is what decides what to fix first.*

Unclear requirements costs the most hours by a wide margin, at 43.8% of all rework time. That is the fix-first target, not because it is the most interesting cause, but because it is the biggest number on the chart.

This is where a lot of teams get the fix wrong: they invest in better code review tooling because "missed in review" feels like an engineering problem, while the actual biggest cost is a product and planning problem that no amount of review tooling touches.

**TRY IT**

**1. Given this breakdown, would investing in a stronger code review checklist meaningfully reduce Cascade’s total rework hours? Defend your answer with the numbers.**

**Suggested answer:** Only marginally. Missed in review accounts for 28 of 105 total hours, about 27%. Even eliminating it entirely would leave unclear requirements, the largest single cause at 46 hours, completely untouched. The bigger lever is tightening how requirements get written and confirmed before work starts, not review tooling.
