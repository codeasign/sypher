Relay logged every engineer-hour across the quarter into four buckets: writing code, reviewing others' code, meetings, and cross-team support. The totals: coding 620, reviewing 210, meetings 340, support 180, out of 1350 total hours.

| Category | Hours | Share of total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coding | 620 | 45.9% |
| Reviewing | 210 | 15.6% |
| Meetings | 340 | 25.2% |
| Support | 180 | 13.3% |

![Donut chart showing how the Relay team’s total weekly hours split across coding, reviewing, meetings, and support](https://syhpher-next-datastore-gvaf.b-cdn.net/svgs/measuring-developer-productivity/where-hours-go.svg)

*Shown as a donut chart because this is composition, how one whole (the team’s total hours) splits into parts, not a comparison across separate items.*

Meetings take up 25.2% of the team’s total hours, more than reviewing and support combined. That is the number that should end the "why does everything take so long" conversation with actual evidence instead of a feeling.

This breakdown is a lagging indicator, not a lever you pull directly. You cannot order a team to "have fewer meeting hours" the way you can order a code freeze. You use this chart to find which meetings are the biggest chunk of that slice, and cut those specifically.

**TRY IT**

**1. Reviewing takes up a smaller share than meetings, yet PR cycle time (Module 2) was the team’s biggest complaint. Is that a contradiction?**

**Suggested answer:** No. Cycle time measures elapsed wall-clock time a PR waits, not hours spent reviewing. A PR can wait 40 hours for review while the actual reviewing takes 20 minutes, if the reviewer is busy in meetings during that window. Low reviewing hours and high cycle time can coexist and often point at the same root cause: meeting load crowding out quick review turnaround.

**2. If you could move 5% of total hours from one category to another, which move would most likely also improve cycle time from Module 2?**

**Suggested answer:** Move hours from meetings to reviewing. Since Module 2 traced the cycle time improvement to faster review turnaround, giving engineers more protected time for reviewing (funded by fewer or shorter meetings) directly attacks the same bottleneck, rather than adding more coding time, which does not touch the queueing problem.
