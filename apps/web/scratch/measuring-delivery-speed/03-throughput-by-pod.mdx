Throughput counts shipped features per pod across the quarter. It answers a different question than lead time: not how fast any one change moves, but how much each pod actually delivers.

| Pod | Checkout | Search | Notifications |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Features shipped | 14 | 9 | 11 |

Checkout shipped 14 features, the most of the three pods, against Search’s 9 and Notifications' 11.

![Bar chart comparing features shipped across the Checkout, Search, and Notifications pods](https://syhpher-next-datastore-gvaf.b-cdn.net/svgs/measuring-delivery-speed/throughput-by-pod.svg)

*Shown as a bar chart because the point is comparing three pods against each other, not a trend over time.*

Comparing pods by raw feature count only works if the features are roughly comparable in size, which is rarely true. Checkout might simply own smaller, more numerous features. Northbeam uses this chart to spot a pod that is stuck, not to rank pods against each other on effort.

**TRY IT**

**1. Notifications shipped the fewest features. Name one legitimate reason that has nothing to do with the pod being slower.**

**Suggested answer:** Notifications features might be larger and more complex on average (cross-cutting infrastructure work, for instance), or the pod might be carrying more production support load that quietly eats into feature time. Raw feature count does not normalize for size or hidden work, so a low count alone does not prove low output.
