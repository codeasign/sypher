Leakage rate is the percentage of all defects found (in testing plus in production) that were found in production, defects testing missed. It is the mirror image of defect detection percentage from a different course: what testing missed instead of what it caught.

| Release | R1 | R2 | R3 | R4 | R5 | R6 | R7 | R8 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Leakage rate (%) | 22 | 19 | 24 | 17 | 14 | 11 | 9 | 7 |

Releases 1 through 3 averaged 21.7% leakage. Releases 6 through 8 averaged 9%, a drop of 12.7 points. Tidewater’s change: they added a staging environment that mirrors production configuration exactly, closing a gap that had been letting environment-specific bugs through untested.

![Line chart of Tidewater defect leakage rate percentage across 8 releases, falling from 22% to 7%](https://syhpher-next-datastore-gvaf.b-cdn.net/svgs/measuring-defect-leakage/leakage-rate-trend.svg)

*Shown as a line chart because the point is the trend across 8 releases, not a single comparison.*

A falling leakage rate is good news on its own, more so than most metrics in this course family, since it is hard to improve this number without genuinely catching more defects before release. But it says nothing about how bad the defects that still get through are, which is exactly what the next two modules cover.

**TRY IT**

**1. Leakage rate fell steadily from 22% to 7% over 8 releases. Would you expect it to keep falling toward 0% indefinitely with continued investment?**

**Suggested answer:** No. Some leakage is close to unavoidable: production-scale traffic patterns, real user data combinations, and third-party service behavior can surface conditions no pre-release environment fully replicates. A realistic floor exists somewhere above 0%, and a team reporting leakage suspiciously close to zero is worth double-checking for under-reporting rather than assuming a perfect testing process.
