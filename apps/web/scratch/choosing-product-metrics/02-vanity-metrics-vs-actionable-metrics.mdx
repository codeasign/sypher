---
title: "Vanity Metrics vs. Actionable Metrics"
order: 2
---

A vanity metric goes up no matter what you do and tells you nothing to act on. Signups climb because of ad spend, not because the product works. Page views climb because the app got chattier, not more useful. An actionable metric changes your next decision.

| Metric | Type |
| --- | --- |
| Signups | Vanity |
| Page views | Vanity |
| Weekly Active Users | Proxy |
| Weekly Active Projects (task completed) | Actionable |
| NPS | Actionable |
| Churned accounts | Actionable |
| MRR | Lagging outcome |
| Support tickets closed | Actionable |

![Donut chart of the eight metrics on Loomwork's old dashboard classified as vanity, proxy, actionable, or lagging outcome](https://syhpher-next-datastore-gvaf.b-cdn.net/svgs/choosing-product-metrics/metric-types-mix.svg)

*Shown as a donut chart because this is composition, what share of the old dashboard was actually vanity noise.*

2 of the 8 metrics on Loomwork's old dashboard were pure vanity, 1 was a proxy metric (real activity, but not directly tied to value), and only 4 were genuinely actionable day to day, with 1 lagging outcome metric (MRR) that confirms results after the fact but can't be acted on directly. A dashboard that is a quarter vanity metrics is not measuring the product, it is measuring activity.

**TRY IT**

**1. Loomwork also tracked "total accounts created (all time)". Is that vanity, proxy, or actionable, and why?**

**Suggested answer:** Vanity. It only grows, never reflects current health, and no decision changes based on it going up. A team could stop shipping entirely and the number would still climb.

**2. A metric called "average session length" is proposed for the dashboard. What question would you ask before classifying it?**

**Suggested answer:** What is happening during that time. Long sessions could mean deep, valuable work, or they could mean the user is confused and struggling to find something. Without knowing what the time is spent on, the metric alone does not tell you whether to be happy or worried, which is a sign it needs a companion metric before it is actionable.
