Instead of arguing about which metric "feels" more meaningful, Loomwork checked which one actually moved the same direction as revenue, month over month, across the whole dataset.

| Change | Feb | Mar | Apr | May | Jun |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MRR direction | up | up | up | up | up |
| WAU direction | up | up | down | up | down |
| WAP direction | up | up | up | up | up |

MRR rose every single month, 5 out of 5 month-over-month changes. WAU matched that direction in 3 of those 5 months (60%), dropping in April and June even as revenue rose. WAP matched revenue's direction in 5 of 5 months (100%), a perfect record.

![Bar chart comparing how often WAU and WAP moved in the same direction as revenue month over month](https://syhpher-next-datastore-gvaf.b-cdn.net/svgs/choosing-product-metrics/directional-agreement.svg)

*Shown as a bar chart because this is a direct comparison between two candidate metrics on one score, not a trend.*

That single number, 100% directional agreement versus 60%, is what actually settled the argument at Loomwork. WAP became the north star metric; WAU stayed on the dashboard as a secondary awareness metric, not the headline.

**TRY IT**

**1. A third candidate metric, "average time in app per session," rose in Feb, Mar, Apr, fell in May, and rose in Jun. Revenue rose every month (Feb through Jun, 5 changes). What is its directional agreement percentage with revenue?**

**Suggested answer:** 4 out of 5 months matched (Feb, Mar, Apr, Jun all rose alongside revenue; May fell while revenue rose), so 80%. That beats WAU's 60% but does not match WAP's 100%.

**2. Suppose a metric had 100% directional agreement with revenue but only existed for the last 2 months of data instead of all 6. Would you trust it as much as WAP's 100% score?**

**Suggested answer:** No. Two data points agreeing is far weaker evidence than five, since a short run can agree by chance. The strength of a directional-agreement score depends on how many independent changes it was measured across, not just the percentage itself.
