Time to green is the minutes between a push and the pipeline reporting a stable, trustworthy passing result, including any retries needed along the way.

| Time | <5m | 5-15m | 15-30m | 30-60m | 60m+ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Runs | 61 | 84 | 38 | 14 | 6 |

![Histogram of Driftwood CI time to green in minutes, from push to a stable passing pipeline](https://syhpher-next-datastore-gvaf.b-cdn.net/svgs/measuring-test-stability/time-to-green.svg)

*Shown as a histogram because the point is the distribution of how long a pipeline takes to go green, not a comparison of separate categories.*

71.4% of runs reach green within 15 minutes, but 20 runs, a real and costly minority, take over 30 minutes, some over an hour. Every one of those long tails traces back to a flaky retry cycle, exactly the problem Modules 2 and 3 are about.

This distribution is the dollar-and-cents version of the flaky rate story. A 2% flaky rate sounds small until you see it concentrated into a long tail of engineers waiting nearly an hour for a pipeline that should have taken five minutes, on the days their run happened to hit one of the surviving flaky tests.

**TRY IT**

**1. If Driftwood fully fixed the worst flaky test from Module 3, which part of this histogram would you expect to shrink the most?**

**Suggested answer:** The long tail, specifically the 30-60m and 60m+ buckets. Those long times are driven by retry cycles from flaky failures, not by genuinely slow tests, so removing the single biggest source of flaky retries should pull the most extreme outliers back toward the 5-15m bucket where most runs already land, shrinking the tail more than it changes the bulk of the distribution.
