Flaky rate is the percentage of pipeline runs that had at least one test fail, then pass on retry with no code change, in other words, a false alarm.

| Day | D1 | D2 | D3 | D4 | D5 | D6 | D7 | D8 | D9 | D10 | D11 | D12 | D13 | D14 | D15 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Day | D16 | D17 | D18 | D19 | D20 | D21 | D22 | D23 | D24 | D25 | D26 | D27 | D28 | D29 | D30 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rate (%) | 7 | 5 | 6 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 2 |

The first five days averaged 16.6% flaky rate. The last five days averaged 2.4%, a fall of 14.2 points. Driftwood’s fix was not a tooling change: they instituted a rule that any test failing twice in one week without a related code change gets quarantined out of the main suite until someone fixes it.

![Line chart of Driftwood CI flaky test rate percentage across 30 days, falling from 18% to 2%](https://syhpher-next-datastore-gvaf.b-cdn.net/svgs/measuring-test-stability/flaky-rate-trend.svg)

*Shown as a line chart because the point is the trend across 30 days, not a single comparison.*

This is one of the highest-value trends a CI-owning team can chase, since flakiness compounds. Every flaky run trains engineers to distrust red pipelines, and a team that distrusts its pipeline eventually stops reacting to genuine failures too.
