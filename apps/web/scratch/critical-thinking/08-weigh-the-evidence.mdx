---
title: "Weigh the evidence"
order: 8
---

Two things happening at the same time is the weakest kind of evidence there is, and it is the kind people quote most.

Nina reads that a city added bike lanes and, in the same year, road accidents dropped. "Bike lanes save lives," she tells her friends. Maybe. But the city may also have lowered the speed limit, or fewer people drove because fuel got expensive. The report shows two things happened together. It does not say one caused the other.

Weighing evidence means asking how strong each piece is, and how much it really proves.

## Correlation is not cause

Two things moving together does not mean one makes the other happen. Ice cream sales and sunburns both rise in summer. Ice cream does not cause sunburn. The weather causes both.

When you hear "X leads to Y", ask: could Y cause X? Could a third thing cause both? Could it be chance?

## Not all evidence is equal

From weaker to stronger:

* A single story ("it worked for my cousin")
* A group of stories with no comparison
* A comparison between similar groups
* Repeated results from independent studies

A story is not useless. It can show you what to test. It just should not be the last step.

## Small samples and missing numbers

"Nine out of ten users love it" sounds strong. If only ten users were asked, and all were the company's friends, it is not.

Bad version: "Our survey proves customers want a mobile app."

Better version: "Sixty customers answered, out of two thousand emailed. They lean toward mobile. Before we build, let us try a short test with a wider group."

The better version says what the data shows, how limited it is, and what to do next. It does not overclaim or throw the data away.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. A school starts a breakfast program and grades rise. Write two other explanations.

**Suggested answer:** "A new teacher joined that year" or "Attendance improved for another reason." Any real alternative shows you separated correlation from cause.

Exercise 2. Someone says, "I tried that supplement and felt great, so it works." Write one fair reply.

**Suggested answer:** "I am glad you feel better. It would help to know whether people who took a fake pill felt better too."

Exercise 3. A report says, "Complaints fell 50 percent." What number would you ask for?

**Suggested answer:** "How many complaints was that? Going from 4 to 2 is very different from going from 400 to 200."
