Speed comes from regular practice, not from a single session. Fifteen minutes a day for a few weeks will show more change than a weekend course.

## A simple practice plan

Do this for four weeks:

* Week 1: Measure your baseline on two text types. Practice previews on everything you read.
* Week 2: Add reading in groups and stop going back. Use a card to block regressions for ten minutes a day.
* Week 3: Add a pacer, set slightly above your comfortable speed. Loosen the inner voice on easy text.
* Week 4: Add skimming and scanning on purpose. Add a summary after each section.

Each day, spend 15 minutes on practice with a real text you care about, then write a few lines on what you understood.

## Measure every week

Once a week, repeat the one minute test on similar text, and check your comprehension. Record the results in your log. If your speed goes up and comprehension stays about the same, you are on track. If comprehension falls, go back to the last comfortable speed.

## What to expect

Many people improve by a fifth to a third in a few weeks with careful practice. Some gain less, and that is normal. Your real gain also comes from choosing what to read, skimming what does not need close reading, and stopping when you have what you need.

## Build the habit

Read at the same time every day, in the same place. Turn off notifications. Keep a book or long article ready. If you miss a day, restart the next day.

## Cheat sheet

* Measure speed and comprehension first.
* Speed and understanding trade off, so aim for honest gains.
* Preview before you read.
* Read in groups of words, and stop going back.
* Use a pacer slightly above your comfortable speed.
* Loosen the inner voice, but do not fight it on hard text.
* Skim to get the gist, scan to find a fact.
* Ask questions, summarize, and review.
* Practice a little every day.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. Write your reading practice slot and text for the next week.

**Suggested answer:** "Fifteen minutes after breakfast, reading the industry newsletter, with a pen as a pacer."

Exercise 2. Write your weekly test and what you will do if comprehension drops.

**Suggested answer:** "Each Sunday, a one minute test and a five point check. If I get fewer than four points, I go back to last week's pace."

Exercise 3. Write one type of text you will stop reading closely and start skimming.

**Suggested answer:** "Promotional emails and long email threads. I will skim for the action and the date."
