title: "Measure where you are"
order: 2
---

Before you try to read faster, find out how fast you read now. Without a starting point, you cannot tell whether anything works.

Here is how to measure it in about five minutes.

## Take a reading speed test

Choose a text you have not read, such as an article of a few pages. Set a timer for exactly one minute. Read at your normal pace, the way you would read for understanding. When the minute ends, mark where you stopped.

Count the words on a few full lines, find the average per line, and multiply by the number of lines you read. For a quick estimate, count the words in ten lines, divide by ten, and multiply by the number of lines.

Example: 11 words per line, 22 lines read. That is about 242 words per minute.

## Check your understanding

Speed means nothing without comprehension. After reading, close the text and write down the main points from memory. Then check. Give yourself a score: how many of the five main points did you get?

Bad version: "I read that in six minutes, so I am fast."

Better version: "I read 1,450 words in six minutes, about 240 per minute. I could state four of the five main points."

What makes it better: you now have a speed and a comprehension score, so you can compare later.

## Test different types of text

Your speed will differ by material. A light novel might be 300 words per minute or more. A dense technical document may be 150. Test two or three types, since each needs a different approach.

## Keep your baseline

Write the numbers in a notebook or a phone note, with the date. You will return to them in the last lesson.

## Do not chase a number

A reading speed is a tool for tracking progress, not a score to show off. A faster number with lower understanding is a step backward.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. Measure your speed on one text and write the result.

**Suggested answer:** "I read 21 lines with about 10 words each in one minute, so 210 words per minute. I recalled three of five main points."

Exercise 2. Write your comprehension check for a report you read.

**Suggested answer:** "After closing it, I listed the goal, the main finding, the two recommendations, and the deadline. I checked and missed the second recommendation."

Exercise 3. Choose two text types you read often and note them in your log.

**Suggested answer:** "Work emails and long news articles. I will test each separately."
