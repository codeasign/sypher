Review load counts completed reviews per engineer over the same 8 weeks, regardless of PR size or how long each review took.

| Engineer | Diego | Hana | Owen | Rasha | Leif | Mei |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reviews | 22 | 15 | 19 | 11 | 26 | 17 |

Leif completed 26 reviews; Rasha completed 11, a 2.4x gap between the busiest and quietest reviewer on a 6-person team.

![Bar chart comparing PRs reviewed per engineer across 6 Fenwick engineers](https://syhpher-next-datastore-gvaf.b-cdn.net/svgs/measuring-code-review/review-load.svg)

*Shown as a bar chart because the point is comparing six engineers’ review load against each other, not a trend.*

A gap this size is not automatically a problem. Leif might simply own the modules that get touched most. It becomes a problem when it is invisible: nobody on the team could name this number before Fenwick started tracking it, which meant nobody could ask whether it was sustainable.

**TRY IT**

**1. Rasha reviewed the fewest PRs. List two very different explanations for that number, one concerning and one not.**

**Suggested answer:** Not concerning: Rasha works in an area of the codebase with fewer PRs to review, or was out for part of the 8 weeks. Concerning: Rasha is being routed around because reviews take too long or are seen as unhelpful, meaning the low number reflects an avoidance pattern rather than low opportunity.

**2. Would you fix a 2.4x review load gap by assigning reviews evenly across all six engineers? Why or why not?**

**Suggested answer:** Not necessarily. Even assignment ignores codebase ownership and expertise, someone forced to review unfamiliar code reviews it slower and worse. The better fix is checking whether the gap tracks ownership (fine) or avoidance (not fine) before changing the assignment process at all.
