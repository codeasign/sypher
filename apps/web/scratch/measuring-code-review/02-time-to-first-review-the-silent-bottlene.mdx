Time to first review counts the hours between a PR opening and any reviewer leaving the first comment. It says nothing about review quality, only about how long the PR sat untouched.

| Week | W1 | W2 | W3 | W4 | W5 | W6 | W7 | W8 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hours | 9.5 | 8.2 | 10.1 | 7.4 | 6.8 | 5.9 | 5.2 | 4.8 |

Week 1 averaged 9.5 hours before anyone looked at a new PR. By week 8 that fell to 4.8 hours, a drop of 4.7 hours. Fenwick’s change: they added a Slack bot that pings a random available reviewer the moment a PR opens, instead of relying on someone noticing it in a dashboard.

![Line chart of Fenwick time to first review in hours across 8 weeks, falling from about 9.5 to under 5](https://syhpher-next-datastore-gvaf.b-cdn.net/svgs/measuring-code-review/time-to-first-review.svg)

*Shown as a line chart because the point is the trend across 8 weeks, not a single comparison.*

A falling time-to-first-review number is close to a pure process win, since it does not depend on PR size or difficulty the way review outcome does. It is one of the cleanest signals in this whole course precisely because it is hard to game without actually reviewing faster.

**TRY IT**

**1. Week 3 ticks up to 10.1 hours after week 2’s 8.2. Before assuming the bot broke, what would you check first?**

**Suggested answer:** Check whether week 3 had unusually high PR volume or a public holiday reducing available reviewers. A spike in supply-demand mismatch explains a one-week blip without implicating the tooling, and checking it first avoids a wasted debugging session on the bot itself.

**2. A team with time-to-first-review under 2 hours could still have a slow overall cycle time. What number from a different course module would you need to confirm that?**

**Suggested answer:** You would need PR cycle time (open to merge), not just first review. A fast first comment followed by several slow rounds of change requests and re-review still adds up to a long total cycle time, so first-review speed alone cannot confirm the PR actually merged quickly.
