Ledger runs a static analyzer on every merge. Over the quarter it flagged 6 critical issues, 24 major issues, and 58 minor issues, 88 total.

| Severity | Count | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Critical | 6 | 6.8% |
| Major | 24 | 27.3% |
| Minor | 58 | 65.9% |

![Donut chart of Ledger static analysis issues split across critical, major, and minor severity](https://syhpher-next-datastore-gvaf.b-cdn.net/svgs/measuring-code-quality/issue-severity-mix.svg)

*Shown as a donut chart because this is composition, how the whole set of flagged issues splits by severity.*

Critical issues are only 6.8% of the total, but they are the 6 that matter most. The other 93.2%, mostly minor style and lint-level flags, is useful signal in aggregate but should never compete for attention with a critical finding.

The mistake this chart helps a team avoid: treating "we closed 40 static analysis tickets this sprint" as progress, when 38 of them were minor and the 2 critical ones sat untouched. Total ticket count and severity-weighted priority are different measures of progress, and only one of them is the one that protects the business.

**TRY IT**

**1. A sprint report says "45 static analysis issues resolved this sprint, up from 30 last sprint." What is missing from that sentence that this module’s data says you should demand?**

**Suggested answer:** The severity breakdown of what got resolved. Resolving 45 minor issues while 2 critical ones from last sprint remain open is worse progress than resolving 15 issues if all 6 critical ones are among them. A raw resolved-count without severity weighting can look like improvement while the actual risk profile stays flat or worsens.
