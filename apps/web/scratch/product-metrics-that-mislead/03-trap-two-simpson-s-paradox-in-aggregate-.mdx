---
title: "Trap Two: Simpson's Paradox in Aggregate Retention"
order: 3
---

Nettleback's aggregate week-6 retention jumped from 48% to 60% between two periods, a huge apparent win. Both of the two user segments that make up that aggregate, power users and casual users, individually got worse over the same stretch.

| Segment | Period 1 users | Period 1 retained | Period 1 rate | Period 2 users | Period 2 retained | Period 2 rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Power users | 200 | 160 | 80% | 550 | 429 | 78% |
| Casual users | 800 | 320 | 40% | 450 | 171 | 38% |

![Stacked bar chart of Nettleback user base composition by power users and casual users in two periods, showing the mix shifting heavily toward power users](https://syhpher-next-datastore-gvaf.b-cdn.net/svgs/product-metrics-that-mislead/mix-shift.svg)

*Shown as a stacked bar chart because the point is how the composition of the user base shifted between the two periods, not a single trend.*

Power-user retention fell from 80% to 78%. Casual-user retention fell from 40% to 38%. Yet aggregate retention rose from 48% to 60%, because power users, who retain far better than casual users at both points in time, grew from 20% of the user base to 55%.

The aggregate number did not lie about its own arithmetic. It lied about the story, because the mix of who is in the average shifted enough to overwhelm two real declines. Reporting "retention is up" off the aggregate alone would have missed that the product got worse for both types of user.

**TRY IT**

**1. Aggregate retention rose 12 points (48% to 60%) even though both segments declined 2 points each. What single change in the data is responsible?**

**Suggested answer:** The composition shift: power users (who retain much higher than casual users in both periods) grew from 20% to 55% of the user base. Since the aggregate is a weighted average, weighting more heavily toward the higher-retaining segment raises the aggregate even while both segments individually decline.

**2. What check would have caught this before it was reported to leadership as a retention win?**

**Suggested answer:** Breaking the aggregate down by segment before reporting it, the same table shown in this module. Any time an aggregate metric is reported, checking it against its major underlying segments catches a mix-shift illusion that the single number alone cannot reveal.
