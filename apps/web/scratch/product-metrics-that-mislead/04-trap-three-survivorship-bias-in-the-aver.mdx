---
title: "Trap Three: Survivorship Bias in the Average Rating"
order: 4
---

Nettleback reported an average in-app rating of 4.6 out of 5 for a quarter. That average only ever included users still active enough to be asked, current users. Churned users were never in the sample.

| Group | Users | Average rating |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Active users (surveyed in-app) | 850 | 4.6 |
| Churned users (exit survey) | 150 | 2.1 |

![Bar chart comparing Nettleback's average rating counting only currently active users against a blended average that includes churned users' exit survey ratings](https://syhpher-next-datastore-gvaf.b-cdn.net/svgs/product-metrics-that-mislead/active-only-vs-blended-rating.svg)

*Shown as a bar chart because this is a direct comparison between two ways of computing the same average, not a trend.*

Blending both groups: (850 x 4.6 + 150 x 2.1) / (850 + 150) = 4.2, a full 0.4 points lower than the reported 4.6.

The 4.6 was not a false number, it was an honest average of the wrong population: the people who liked the product enough to still be using it. Anyone who disliked it enough to leave was excluded from the sample by definition, which is exactly what survivorship bias means.

**TRY IT**

**1. If churned users' average rating had been 4.0 instead of 2.1, much closer to the active users' 4.6, would the survivorship bias problem still matter?**

**Suggested answer:** It would matter less in this specific case, since the two populations would agree more closely and the blended average would land close to 4.6 anyway. The bias is still present in principle (churned users are still excluded from the "active only" number by construction), but its practical impact shrinks when the excluded group does not actually feel differently.

**2. Name one other Nettleback metric, besides average rating, that would likely suffer from the same survivorship bias if computed only from current users.**

**Suggested answer:** Average session length, feature satisfaction scores, or NPS collected only through the app, any metric gathered by asking currently active users would systematically exclude the people who disliked the product enough to already leave, the same structural bias as the rating example.
