You cannot manage time well without checking how it went. A short weekly review keeps the habits alive and shows what to change.

## The 20 minute weekly review

Do it at the same time each week, perhaps Friday afternoon or Sunday evening.

1. Look back. What did I get done? Where did my time go? What surprised me?
2. Check the top three. Did I finish the most important things? If not, why?
3. Clear the loose ends. Move unfinished tasks to next week, or drop them.
4. Look ahead. Check the calendar for the next week. Note deadlines, meetings, and a place for focus blocks.
5. Choose the priorities. Write your top three for the week, and top three for Monday.

## A short example

Elena does her review on Friday at 4:00. She notices she spent three hours on unplanned requests, which delayed her main report. She decides to block two hours on Monday morning for the report, and to tell the team she will answer requests at noon.

Next Friday, she checks. The report is done, and she gets home earlier.

## Adjust, do not punish

If a week went badly, treat it as information. Ask what got in the way and what would make next week 10 percent better. Small changes add up.

## Keep some time for rest

Rest is part of the plan. A schedule without breaks, sleep, and time for people usually falls apart. Protect at least one block each week that is simply yours.

## Cheat sheet

* Track your time for a week to see the truth.
* Choose three priorities each day.
* Put important work in your best hours, and leave gaps.
* Estimate with your history, parts, and a buffer.
* Start small, use a timer.
* Protect focus with set times and blocks.
* Say a clear, kind no.
* Tame meetings, email, and routines.
* Review every week.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. Schedule your weekly review. Write the day and time.

**Suggested answer:** "Fridays at 4:00 for 20 minutes, in my calendar as a recurring event."

Exercise 2. Write one thing you will change next week, based on this course.

**Suggested answer:** "I will close my chat from 9 to 11 every morning to finish my main task first."
