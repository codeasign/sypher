There are two people on the same team with the same workload. One leaves at five with the important work done. The other is still at the desk at eight, replying to messages and feeling behind.

The difference is rarely intelligence or effort. The second person works just as hard. They just spend their hours on whatever arrives next, while the first person decides in advance where the hours go.

Time management is not about squeezing more into every day. It is about choosing what deserves your time, protecting it, and being honest about how long things take. You cannot make more hours, but you can stop losing them without noticing.

This course teaches that in nine parts:

* Finding out where your time actually goes
* Choosing what matters most
* Planning a day that can survive real life
* Estimating how long tasks take
* Starting when you do not feel like it
* Protecting your focus from interruptions
* Saying no without damaging relationships
* Running meetings, email, and routines so they do not eat the day
* Reviewing each week and adjusting

Each lesson is short and ends with a TRY IT of two or three exercises. Do the exercises with your real calendar and real tasks, and you will feel the effect within a week. The suggested answers are examples, so replace them with your own details.

You do not need special software. A notebook or a plain calendar is enough. Tools do not fix time problems. Decisions do.
