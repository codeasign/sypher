Knowing that 250 people activated is only half the picture. When they activated, relative to signing up, changes what Fernway should do about the users who have not yet.

| Days to first check-in | 0-1 day | 2-3 days | 4-7 days | 8+ days |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Activated users | 110 | 70 | 45 | 25 |

![Histogram of days between signup and first check-in among 250 activated Fernway users, most activating within a day](https://syhpher-next-datastore-gvaf.b-cdn.net/svgs/measuring-activation/time-to-activate.svg)

*Shown as a histogram because the point is the distribution of how long activation took, not a single average.*

110 of 250 activated users, 44%, activated within a day of signing up. Only 25 activated users, 10%, took 8 or more days.

That shape says activation, when it happens, happens fast. It reframes the goal for the users who have not activated yet: a nudge on day 2 or 3 is working with the grain of the data, a nudge on day 10 is trying to revive something that has likely already gone cold.

**TRY IT**

**1. Based on the bucket table, what share of activated users took 4 or more days (the "4-7 days" and "8+ days" buckets combined)?**

**Suggested answer:** (45 + 25) / 250 = 70 / 250 = 28.0%. Just over a quarter of activations happen after the first few days, which is still worth designing reminders around even though most activation is fast.

**2. If Fernway wanted to time a single reminder email to catch the largest number of not-yet-activated users at the moment they are statistically most likely to still activate, which bucket boundary would you target and why?**

**Suggested answer:** Around day 2, right at the edge of the "0-1 day" bucket. That is where the largest single group (110 of 250) activates, so a nudge landing just after day 1 is timed to catch the next-largest wave (the "2-3 days" bucket, 70 users) before they drift into the longer, smaller tail.
