Every signup passes through the same sequence of steps on the way to being counted as activated.

| Step | Signed up | Completed profile | Created first habit | Logged first check-in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Users remaining | 500 | 410 | 340 | 250 |

![Bar chart of Fernway signup cohort narrowing across four activation funnel stages from 500 signups to 250 activated users](https://syhpher-next-datastore-gvaf.b-cdn.net/svgs/measuring-activation/activation-funnel.svg)

*Shown as a bar chart because this compares counts across four distinct stages, not a trend over time.*

From signup to completed profile: 82%. From completed profile to first habit created: 82.9%. From first habit created to first check-in logged, the activation step itself: 73.5%. Overall, 50% of the original 500 signups activated.

The biggest single drop is not where most teams assume. Signup to profile loses 18% of the cohort, but first-habit to first-check-in, the very last step, loses 26.5%, meaning a user can do almost everything right and still fall away before ever getting real value.

**TRY IT**

**1. Of the 340 users who created a first habit, 250 went on to log a first check-in. What is that step's conversion rate, and is it Fernway's strongest or weakest step?**

**Suggested answer:** 250/340 = 73.5%. Comparing all three steps (82.0%, 82.9%, 73.5%), this is the weakest step, the one losing the largest share of users who reach it.

**2. A teammate says "activation is 68%" for Fernway. Using the numbers in this module, is that plausible, and what would you ask before trusting it?**

**Suggested answer:** Not plausible against this cohort, activation here is 50% (250/500). Before trusting a different number, ask what "activation" is being defined as, since a looser definition (like "completed profile" instead of "logged a first check-in") would produce a higher, more flattering, but less meaningful number.
