---
title: "Decide faster when you can"
order: 8
---

The most common mistake with decisions is spending the same energy on all of them. Choosing a lunch place does not deserve an hour. Choosing a house does.

Fast decisions are a skill, and they free up time and mental energy for the ones that matter.

## Sort decisions by two questions

Is it reversible? Is the impact large? If a choice is easy to undo and has low impact, decide in minutes. If it is hard to undo and has high impact, slow down.

## Set simple rules for small choices

* For low-stakes shopping, choose the second-cheapest of the top-rated three.
* For a restaurant, take turns choosing.
* For daily clothing, pick from a short set.

Rules protect your attention. Keep them light so they can be broken when needed.

## Use a good enough standard

Some people try to find the best option every time. That takes a lot of effort, and the difference in results is often small. Decide what good enough looks like, and take the first option that meets it.

Bad version: "I have compared 14 blenders for two weeks."

Better version: "It must cost under 80, have good reviews, and fit the shelf. The first one that meets this is fine."

The better version works because it saves your effort for choices that matter more.

## Decide in a time box

Say to yourself, "I will choose by 3 PM." A deadline pushes you to stop looking for something perfect.

## Let others decide small things

Handing minor choices to a colleague or partner is not weakness. It builds trust and frees you up.

## If you cannot decide, flip a coin, and watch your reaction

If a coin toss makes you feel relieved or disappointed, you have learned what you wanted.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. Sort into fast or slow: choosing a birthday gift for a colleague, choosing a college course.

**Suggested answer:** The gift is fast, since it is low impact and easy to undo. The course is slow, since it costs time and money and shapes future options.

Exercise 2. Write a "good enough" rule for buying a backpack.

**Suggested answer:** "Under 60, fits my laptop, and has a return policy. I buy the first one that meets all three."

Exercise 3. Write a time box for deciding what to cook this week.

**Suggested answer:** "Ten minutes on Sunday to pick four meals from my saved list."
