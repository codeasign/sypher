Three people are choosing where to eat. It takes twenty minutes. Nobody cares much about the answer, and everyone leaves a little irritated.

Now picture the same habits applied to a job offer, a flat to rent, or a supplier to trust. The stakes are higher, the time is longer, and the same messy process runs: vague goals, half the facts, a strong feeling, and a rush at the end.

Good decision making is not about being smart or fearless. It is about following a simple sequence so the important choices get more care than the small ones, and so you can live with the result even when it goes badly.

This course teaches that sequence in eight short lessons:

* How to state what you are actually deciding
* How to decide what matters before you compare options
* How to gather enough information without endless research
* How to compare options in a way you can explain
* How to think about risk without freezing
* How to handle feelings and bias
* How to decide quickly when the choice is small or reversible
* How to commit, and then check how it went

Each lesson ends with a TRY IT of two or three exercises and a suggested answer. Try yours first. Real decisions are personal, so there is often no single right answer. Aim for clear reasoning, not a matching sentence.

You can use every lesson on small choices now, like what to buy or when to have a hard conversation. That practice makes the big choices feel less strange when they arrive.
