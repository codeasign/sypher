"What if it goes wrong?"

That question stops many good decisions, and skipping it leads to many bad ones. The skill is to ask it in a way that gets you a plan instead of a knot in your stomach.

## Two questions for every risk

How likely is it? How bad would it be? A risk that is unlikely and mild is not worth worrying about. A risk that is likely and severe needs attention. A risk that is unlikely and severe, such as losing your home, may need insurance or a backup.

## Picture the worst case, in detail

Write it down, calmly: "If this fails, I will lose about 2,000 and three months. I would feel embarrassed, but I could recover by taking freelance work." Being specific shrinks the fear. Vague fears are large, and detailed ones are manageable.

## Ask about the best and the typical case

People often focus only on the worst case. Add the best case and the most likely case. The full picture shows you what you are trading.

## Reduce the risk, do not only avoid it

Bad version: "It is risky, so I will not do it."

Better version: "It is risky. I can lower the risk by testing with a small order first, and keeping three months of savings. Then I will decide."

That change matters because it treats risk as something you can shape.

## Can you undo it?

Reversible choices can be made quickly. Irreversible ones, such as signing a long contract or moving countries, deserve more time. Ask, "How hard would it be to change my mind in six months?"

## Accept that some risk stays

No decision has zero risk. Not deciding also has a cost. If you have done sensible checks, the remaining uncertainty is the price of moving forward.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. You are thinking of leaving a stable job to start a small business. Write the worst case in one specific sentence.

**Suggested answer:** "If it fails, I use about six months of savings and return to a job search, which could take three months."

Exercise 2. Write one way to lower the risk of buying a used car.

**Suggested answer:** "Ask a mechanic to inspect it for a small fee before I pay."

Exercise 3. Your friend says, "It is too risky." Write a question that helps them be specific.

**Suggested answer:** "What is the worst realistic outcome, and could we do something now to make that less likely?"
