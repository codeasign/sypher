---
title: "Compare options in a way you can explain"
order: 5
---

Marta has three job offers. She writes pros and cons for each, and each list has eight items. She reads them twice and feels more confused than before.

Pros and cons lists have a weakness: they treat every item as equal. A small pro and a huge con look the same on paper.

## A simple weighted table

Make a table. List your criteria down the left side, and the options across the top. Give each criterion a weight from 1 to 5 based on how much it matters. Then score each option from 1 to 5 on each criterion. Multiply and add.

Here is a small example for Marta:

| Criterion | Weight | Offer A | Offer B | Offer C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pay | 4 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| Learning | 5 | 5 | 4 | 2 |
| Commute | 3 | 4 | 2 | 3 |

Multiplying and adding gives Offer A 47, Offer B 42, and Offer C 39. Offer A leads because learning mattered most.

## Use it as a check, not a machine

The numbers help you think. If the result feels wrong, do not just override it. Ask what the feeling is telling you. Perhaps you forgot a criterion, or a score is too generous. Adjust the table, or adjust the feeling.

Bad version: "I made a list of pros and cons and A has one more, so A wins."

Better version: "A wins on the things I said matter most. But I noticed I am uneasy about the commute. Let me check that weight."

## Compare against doing nothing

Always include the current state as one option. Sometimes the best choice is to stay, and the table shows it clearly.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. You are choosing between two gyms. List three criteria with weights.

**Suggested answer:** "Distance from home (5), opening hours (4), price (3)." Weights should match how you actually behave, not how you wish you did.

Exercise 2. A table gives you a clear winner, but you feel uneasy. Write one question.

**Suggested answer:** "What did I leave out of the table that my gut is picking up on?"

Exercise 3. Write a sentence that explains a choice to a friend using the table.

**Suggested answer:** "I picked the smaller flat. It was cheaper and closer to work, which were my top two criteria, even though the bigger one had a balcony."
