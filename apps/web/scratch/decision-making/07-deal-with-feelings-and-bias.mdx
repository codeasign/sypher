title: "Deal with feelings and bias"
order: 7
---

Nobody makes decisions with a calm, neutral mind. You make them tired, hungry, excited, worried, and often watched. The goal is not to remove feelings. It is to notice their effect.

## Feelings are information, not orders

If you feel dread about a choice, ask why. It may show a real concern you have not written down. If you feel excitement, ask what is causing it. It may be the option, or it may be the story you tell about it.

## Watch for four common pulls

* Fear of loss: we hate losing more than we like gaining, so we keep bad situations too long.
* The default: we stick with whatever is already set up, such as the current plan or subscription.
* Sunk cost: we continue because we have already spent effort, not because it is still worth it.
* Following the crowd: we choose what others choose, even when their needs differ.

## Give it a night when you can

If a decision is not urgent, sleep on it. Fresh, you will see the same options with less heat. Write down how you feel now, and check it again tomorrow.

Bad version: "I was so angry I just quit."

Better version: "I am angry. I will not send anything today. Tomorrow I will decide whether the problem is the job or the last week."

What makes it better: it separates the feeling from the choice. You can still quit, but you choose it, and it is not triggered.

## Try the friend test

Ask, "What would I tell a friend in the same situation?" We are usually kinder and clearer about other people's choices.

## Check your body state

Do not decide big things when you are exhausted, hungry, or in the middle of a fight. Postponing by a few hours costs little.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. You have paid for a yearly membership you barely use. Write the question that avoids sunk cost.

**Suggested answer:** "If I had not paid, would I sign up now at this price?"

Exercise 2. You feel pressure to accept an offer right away. Write a calm reply.

**Suggested answer:** "Thank you. I would like to give this proper thought. Can I answer by Friday?"

Exercise 3. Write the friend test for a choice you are stuck on.

**Suggested answer:** For example: "If my friend had this offer and these worries, I would tell her to negotiate the start date, not refuse."
