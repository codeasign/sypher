---
title: "Gather enough information, then stop"
order: 4
---

More information makes a decision feel safer. Up to a point, it is. After that point, it only delays you.

Some people research until the option disappears. The flight price goes up, the offer expires, or the other person moves on. Others research nothing and rely on the first review they saw.

## Ask what would change your mind

Before you search, decide what you need to know. Ask, "What information, if I found it, would change my choice?" Look for that first. If nothing would change it, you already have enough.

## Use a mix of sources

* People who have made the same choice, especially a few who regret it
* Numbers, such as costs, times, and ratings, with the sample size
* An expert, who can see risks you cannot

Talk to at least one person who disagrees with the direction you are leaning. They will notice what you skipped.

## Set a limit

Give research a time box. For a small choice, twenty minutes. For a larger one, a week. When the time is up, decide with what you have.

Bad version: "I will keep reading reviews until I feel sure."

Better version: "I will read reviews for 30 minutes, note the three most common complaints, and ask two owners. Then I decide."

This is better because you cannot reach full certainty. You can only reach enough.

## Notice missing information

Ask, "What do I not know, and can I find out cheaply?" A short trial, a visit, or a phone call often teaches more than hours of reading.

## Do not confuse volume with quality

Twenty articles repeating the same claim are one source. Look for independent opinions.

## TRY IT

Exercise 1. You are choosing a laptop. Write the one question that would change your choice.

**Suggested answer:** "Will it run the software I need for work without slowing down?" If yes, other features matter less.

Exercise 2. Set a research limit for buying a sofa costing 800.

**Suggested answer:** "One weekend. Look at three shops, read reviews with more than 100 ratings, and sit on them."

Exercise 3. Write a message asking someone who chose the opposite of you.

**Suggested answer:** "I am thinking of moving to X. You chose to stay. What made you decide that, and would you do it again?"
